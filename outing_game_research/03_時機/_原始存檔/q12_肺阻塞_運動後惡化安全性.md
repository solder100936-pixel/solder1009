## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search didn't turn up anything on exacerbations caused by exertion specifically. What it did find is a body of reviews on whether rehab is safe during and after an exacerbation, and they mostly report few adverse events.
- **Inpatient rehab:** A review of trials on exercise during hospitalization for an exacerbation found one adverse event among 797 participants in the 15 studies that reported them, an arrhythmia that resolved within an hour ([3-8](3-8)). A separate review of early exercise training (within 48 hours of admission) found seven studies reporting adverse events, with one serious event in an exercise group ([7-8](7-8)). Its authors concluded that starting exercise early was safe ([7-2](7-2)).
- **Post-discharge rehab:** A 2024 Thorax meta-analysis of rehab starting within three weeks of discharge reported no intervention-related adverse events ([9-9](9-9)).
- **Cochrane review (2016):** Five studies with 278 participants explicitly recorded adverse events. Four reported none during rehab, and one reported a single serious event ([5-8](5-8)).
- **Network meta-analysis (2020):** Its authors described pulmonary rehab as safe during AECOPD, with only a few minor adverse events ([2-1](2-1), [4-2](4-2)).
The evidence is limited by small samples and inconsistent adverse-event reporting, so these reviews support safety in supervised settings but say little about unsupervised overexertion. For that question, the Healio summary of the early-exercise review and the PubMed abstracts are the most direct starting points.
