## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search didn't return the full text of the Garcia-Aymerich-led paper, but several sources cover the same question.
**Hospitalization (PAC-COPD cohort):** Steps predicted COPD hospitalization only at low activity intensity. Each additional 1000 daily steps at low average intensity was associated with a 20% lower hospitalization risk (HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.67–0.93) after adjusting for potential confounders in the Cox regression model. Among people already active at high intensity, extra steps added no benefit (HR 1.01) (p=0.919). Some listings credit Donaire-Gonzalez et al. as first author, while others list Garcia-Aymerich, so the authorship is inconsistent across sources.
**Mortality (meta-analysis):** A 2023 systematic review of 21 studies (6,076 participants) found that steps per day (per 1000 steps) (hazard ratio (HR) 0.81, 95% CI 0.75-0.88, p<0.001) were negatively associated with mortality risk. The authors note that the quantity and quality of evidence is limited.
**Other supporting evidence:**
- A cohort study of accelerometry and 6-minute walk distance found that exercise tolerance and daily physical activity are important predictors of hospitalization and mortality in COPD, independent of GOLD 2011 classification. Per 1000 steps, the hazard ratio was 0.80 (95% CI 0.70–0.92) for time to death or first COPD-related hospitalization.
- In a US cohort, each 1000 fewer baseline daily steps was linked to a higher rate of acute exacerbations (rate ratio 1.07) after adjusting for FEV1 % predicted and prednisone use for AE in previous year.
- Self-reported activity has also been studied: any level of MVPA is associated with lower risk of all-cause mortality after a COPD hospitalisation.
**Caveat:** Most evidence is observational, and the meta-analysis authors judged the evidence limited. The hospitalization estimate also comes from a single cohort.
