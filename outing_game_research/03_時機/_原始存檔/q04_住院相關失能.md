## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
A systematic review found a pooled hospital-associated disability (HAD) incidence of about 37% among older adults admitted to acute care, but the authors caution that the estimate rests on limited data.
- **Pooled figure:** The preprint version reports a 37% incidence (95% CI 0.31–0.42) across eleven studies. The published abstract lists ten studies and gives 37% (95% CI 0.30–0.43). The study count differs between versions, so check the final PeerJ article before citing the exact number.
- **Data quality:** Only one study reported adequate information to assess HAD incidence, and the methodological quality and risk of bias raised some concerns.
- **Measurement effects:** At the item level, incidence was higher when disability was reported by proxies than when patients reported it. The authors also suggest the overall figure may be inflated, because it can combine disease-related disability with hospital-associated disability.
- **Background prevalence:** An earlier study estimated that the overall prevalence of HAD among older adults admitted to an acute care hospital is 30%.
- **Consequences:** Functional decline during hospitalization is linked to a longer hospital stay (Palmer et al., 1994), a nursing home placement (Fortinsky et al., 1999), and increased mortality (Brown, Friedkin & Inouye, 2004).
The primary source is Giacomino et al., 2023, *PeerJ* 11:e16036, which is open access.
