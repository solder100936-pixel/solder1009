## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The meta-analysis is the best source for a per-day figure. It puts the decline at roughly 0.3% to 0.43% per day, depending on how VO2max is expressed.
- **Pooled rate:** A 2017 systematic review and meta-analysis of 80 strict bed rest studies (1 to 90 days) found VO2max falls linearly with duration. The decline was about -0.3% per day when reported in L/min and -0.43% per day when normalized to body weight (ml/kg/min), with no statistically significant difference between the two. The aim was to assess the effects of strict prolonged bed rest (without countermeasures) on maximal oxygen uptake. The duration of bed rest ranged from 1 to 90 days. VO2max declined linearly across bed rest duration. No statistical difference in the decline was observed between studies reporting L/min and those reporting ml/kg/min. Pre-bed-rest fitness also mattered: 15-26% of the variation in the decline was explained by pre-bed-rest VO2max levels, independent of the duration of bed rest.
- **Longer bed rest, slower daily loss:** A 2006 study of 14-, 42- and 90-day bed rests found that the average daily rates of VO2max, maximal cardiac output and maximal systemic O2 delivery decay were lower with longer bed rest, with the exception of VO2max in the longest bed rest. The authors also described the decline as asymptotic, so it levels off rather than continuing linearly. The same study attributed the decline to two components, one related to cardiovascular deconditioning and one to impairment of peripheral gas exchanges due to a possible muscle function deterioration.
- **Cardiovascular mechanism:** A 1997 study of 42-day head-down tilt bed rest in seven subjects reported VO2max down 16.6%, while maximal cardiac output fell 30.8%, largely from stroke volume changes. The authors noted that the interaction between muscular and cardiovascular changes led to a smaller reduction in VO2max than in cardiovascular O2 transport.
- **Caveats:** The meta-analysis notes that the studies were conducted mainly in young participants, and the sample was over 90% men. Its authors also flagged a large knowledge gap about the causes of decline in VO2max, particularly in postmenopausal women.
I did not run a separate search on the 2017 review's full text, so the per-day figures come from its abstract as indexed in the search results.
