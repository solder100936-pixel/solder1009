## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
I ran one search. It turned up two relevant Taiwanese studies on prolonged mechanical ventilation (PMV) rehabilitation, plus a related ICU dissertation. I didn't find published outcome results for the trial.
- **Retrospective cohort (National Taiwan University Hospital Hsin-Chu, 2021):** This study looked at PMV patients in a 12-bed respiratory care center, a step-down, protocol-driven weaning unit. It used the de Morton Mobility Index to measure post-rehabilitation function and tested whether that function predicted weaning success and survival. Functional status after rehabilitation was a significant parameter tied to weaning success and survival in these patients. The authors also note that studies on functional status after routine pulmonary rehabilitation and weaning or survival outcomes in PMV patients remain limited.
- **Randomized trial (Taichung Tzu Chi Hospital, NCT05154786):** This trial compares early mobilization plus endurance training against usual care in a respiratory care center. Usual care already includes weaning protocols, bed exercises and early mobilization. The trial enrolled patients who had been on mechanical ventilation for at least 21 days, with stable hemodynamics and the ability to follow simple oral commands. Its planned primary outcomes are weaning rate, days of mechanical ventilation, and length of stay in the respiratory care center. The registry lists the status as completed, with about 40 participants, but I found no published results.
- **ICU dissertation (National Taiwan University, 2022):** This work tested chest physiotherapy with early mobilization in ICU patients. The first study found that intensive chest physiotherapy with early mobilization could decrease extubation failure in mechanically ventilated patients in the ICU. The second linked muscle strength before extubation to later walking ability.
For the original question, the strongest direct evidence is the 2021 Hsin-Chu cohort. Its design is retrospective, so it shows association rather than causation. The Taichung trial would be the stronger test once its results are published. I'd suggest checking PubMed or the trial's registry entry for the outcome data.
