## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search didn't turn up a published progressive sitting protocol for ventilator-dependent people with SCI, so there's no specific hour-by-hour guidance here. The results were mostly general SCI seating and sitting-tolerance material.
- **Sitting tolerance in practice:** One case report describes a person with cervical SCI who was able to sit for no more than 30 minutes at a time, no more than two times per day early on, and who within two weeks of using the power wheelchair was sitting for about two hours at a time. This is a single case, not a validated protocol, and it doesn't mention ventilation.
- **Tolerance as a measure:** A SCIRE resource defines sitting tolerance (how long you are able to sit in your chair comfortably), which is useful framing for a progressive plan.
- **Orthostatic and autonomic considerations:** A VA study of a standing wheelchair notes that patients with autonomic dysreflexia (AD) and/or frequent orthostatic hypotension (OH) are potentially those who may benefit the most from regular upright posture. Its training sessions had people stand at least 5 min during every 15 min or more as tolerated, with tolerance judged by blood pressure stability and comfort. Ventilator-dependent people with high-level injuries often have blood pressure instability, so a similar monitored, incremental approach may be relevant, but this study did not involve ventilated participants.
- **Sitting-position support:** A review notes that a person's ability to sit unsupported against gravity can be characterized by 1 of 3 levels (hands-free, hands-dependent, or prop sitting), which matters for judging how long someone can safely sit.
For a real protocol, I'd suggest searching for terms like "tetraplegia ventilator wheelchair tilt-in-space" or "high-level SCI sitting tolerance respiratory" in clinical rehabilitation databases such as PubMed, or consulting the respiratory and rehab teams directly.
