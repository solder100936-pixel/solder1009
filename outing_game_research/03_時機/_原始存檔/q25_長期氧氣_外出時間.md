The search didn't turn up a single headline percentage of long-term oxygen users who are homebound. It did turn up a few relevant surveys.
- **Madrid telephone survey (2019):** This is the closest match to your question. Most respondents carried portable devices in a wheeled cart, and 71% of the participants reported leaving the home for between 1 and 3 h a day. That measures outing duration, not how many people stay home entirely, and it comes from one region.
- **ATS patient survey (2017):** This was a large survey of 1,926 patients with lung disease, and they struggled with insurance coverage, inappropriate equipment, inadequate supply and heavy tanks. The qualitative results include patients describing themselves as mostly homebound, because the oxygen available to me is too heavy to carry or too bulky to wheel, so an outing is more trouble than it's worth. These are individual quotes, not a prevalence figure.
- **Qualitative review of 311 responses:** Its authors reported decreased mobility and increased isolation experienced by users of heavier portable oxygen systems. Another survey described inadequate portable high-flow systems that limited the number of hours that they could leave their home.
- **Supporting context:** One consumer-law piece notes that over 1.5 million adults living in the United States reliant on long term oxygen therapy plan carefully for any trip outside the home. Gas tanks, the most commonly prescribed portable option, only allow for 1.4-2.3 hours of use, dependent on tank size.
For an exact percentage of homebound users, you'd likely need the full ATS survey results or a national study. I didn't find one in this search, so I can't give you a reliable number.
