## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search didn't turn up a single clean percent figure for quadriceps loss during a COPD admission. The studies report absolute changes, and the one with percentages measured a training intervention.
- **Strength falls during admission and keeps falling after discharge.** In a 52-patient prospective study, quadriceps strength dropped by about 1 kg during the stay. By one-month follow-up, lower-limb strength was measured as worse again, and the authors concluded that impairment is greater at 1-month follow-up. (The aims of this study were to analyze physical and functional impairment during hospital stay, with follow-up at one month.)
- **Low in-hospital quadriceps strength predicts slower recovery.** In a 27-patient cohort, patients who did not recover exercise capacity by day 30 had lower quadriceps strength during admission (15 ± 5 vs 22 ± 6 kgf), and quadriceps strength was the only strength measure linked to recovery. The authors suggested early rehabilitation to improve quadriceps strength.
- **Resistance training during admission helped.** In a randomized trial of 40 patients, enhanced quadriceps force was seen at discharge (+9.7 +/- 16% in the training group; -1 +/- 13% in control subjects; P = 0.05) and at 1 month follow-up in the patients who trained.
- **Physical activity after discharge is the less-studied piece.** A three-month follow-up study of daily physical activity and quadriceps strength was described as needed, because longitudinal data beyond 30 days were lacking. I did not retrieve its results.
For a precise percent loss over one month, you would need the full text of the 2016 *Respiratory Care* study or a dedicated search for the percentage change in quadriceps strength.
