## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search turned up several relevant studies, though none is a large survey specifically about outings.
- **Outside-the-home risk (most relevant):** A study of 12 ALS patients on home ventilation looked at health problems during outings, and split them by stage: leaving the house, being out, and returning. Problems on leaving were mostly tied to the home environment, such as obstacles or tight spaces. Problems while out were mostly tied to the outside environment or caregivers, and problems on the way back were mostly tied to caregivers. The authors recommended skilled caregivers who can handle unexpected situations during outings, and support for patients and caregivers who are fatigued afterward. This is an older study (indexed in 2005), and its sample is very small. (index 9-3 to 9-10)
- **Device failures:** A retrospective review of 248 on-call logs from an Ontario ventilator equipment pool (2011–2012) recorded 188 logs with patient incidents, and device failures were the most common type. (index 7-7, 7-9, 7-10)
- **Adverse events in general:** A 2022 nursing-perspective study found that staff named patient transportation, suctioning, and tracheostomy decannulation as the main causes of adverse events in home ventilation. (index 2-10)
- **Helpline data:** An analysis of calls to a home ventilation helpline found that most technical problems were resolved simply, while some calls with no equipment fault still led to patients being found unwell or admitted to hospital. (index 6-6, 6-7)
- **Data gaps:** Researchers note there are no national registers for ventilator errors in home care, so the data on these events is limited. (index 1-12)
I did not find a large survey that directly measures problems during outings or travel, or ventilator failures specifically outside the home. Those topics may need a targeted search of respiratory or sleep medicine journals.
