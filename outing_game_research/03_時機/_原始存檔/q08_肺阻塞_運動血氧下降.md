## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The sources agree that COPD exertional desaturation (ED) has no single definition, and reported prevalence varies widely with the cutoff used.
- **Definitions vary.** One group defines ED as a 6MWT nadir SpO2 below 90% or a drop of at least 4% ([PMC8538486](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8538486)). Another uses the same 4% drop but with a threshold of under 88% instead ([Foliamedica](https://foliamedica.bg/article_preview.php?id=85983)). The <88% nadir appears in a Maastricht study that examined prevalence at that cutoff ([Maastricht](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/exercise-induced-oxygen-desaturation-in-copd-patients-without-res/)).
- **Prevalence depends on the definition.** In a continuous-monitoring cohort of 421 patients, a 4% fall flagged 71% of participants ([RC journal](https://rc.rcjournal.com/content/68/1/92/tab-pdf)). Only 19.2% of that cohort desaturated during the walk but not at its end ([RC journal](https://rc.rcjournal.com/content/68/1/92/tab-pdf)). Another study reported 39% prevalence of exercise-induced desaturation in COPD patients without resting hypoxemia ([Northumbria/UWS research portal](https://research-portal.uws.ac.uk/en/publications/exercise-induced-oxygen-desaturation-in-copd-patients-without-res/)).
- **Prevalence is uncertain overall.** A review notes that the true prevalence of ED is unclear and that it is more frequent in severe disease ([PMC8538486](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8538486)).
- **Clinical relevance.** Several studies link ED to mortality and hospitalization ([RC journal](https://rc.rcjournal.com/content/68/1/92/tab-pdf)). Baseline SpO2 alone predicts ED poorly, with a ≤95% cutoff showing 81% sensitivity but 49% specificity ([UWS](https://research-portal.uws.ac.uk/en/publications/exercise-induced-oxygen-desaturation-in-copd-patients-without-res/)).
For a precise figure, check which definition a given study used, since the 4% drop criterion alone yields much higher prevalence than the nadir-below-88% criterion.
