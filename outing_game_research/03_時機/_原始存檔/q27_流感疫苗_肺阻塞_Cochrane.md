## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The 2018 Cochrane review (Kopsaftis, Wood-Baker, Poole; CD002733) found that inactivated influenza vaccine reduces COPD exacerbations, but the evidence is thin. The main pooled result is a mean difference of −0.37 in total exacerbations per vaccinated participant versus placebo (95% CI −0.64 to −0.11), based on two RCTs with 180 participants. That result was rated low quality.
The search results don't say the figure is "per patient per year," and I couldn't confirm the follow-up period, so check the full review before citing it that way. The review also reports:
- **Late exacerbations:** The reduction was driven by fewer 'late' exacerbations, occurring after three or four weeks (MD –0.39, 95% CI –0.61 to –0.18; two RCTs, 180 participants).
- **Influenza-related infections:** Vaccination also lowered influenza-related respiratory infections (WMD 0.19, 95% CI 0.07 to 0.48; 2 studies, n=180), per the same evidence summary.
- **Safety:** The injection was associated with short-lived local side effects such as pain at the injection site.
- **Live attenuated add-on:** Adding a live attenuated virus to the inactivated virus did not add any further protection for the participants.
- **Mortality:** Two studies evaluating mortality for influenza vaccine versus placebo were too small to have detected any effect on mortality.
- **Trial scope:** The review included six studies with 2469 participants with COPD, and a further five studies with 4281 older or high-risk participants, a proportion of whom had chronic lung disease. There have been no new trials since 2004.
