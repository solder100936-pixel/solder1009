The search turned up no single standard for training duration, but several programs and surveys give concrete details.
- **Two trained caregivers:** A 2020 survey found that most institutions (78.4%) required two trained caregivers, and nearly all (94%) required caregivers to show they could work independently. Nearly all respondents' institutions (94%) required that caregivers demonstrate independent care; the majority (78.4%) required two trained HMV caregivers. A Canadian-style guideline also calls for at least two family caregivers who must be fully trained in all aspects of the child's care.
- **Training length:** Riley Children's program is the most specific example. It requires a minimum of one 8-hour and two 24-hour caregiver shifts, and requires a minimum of two fully trained caregivers and can train up to four individuals. Other programs describe training as variable: training models vary in duration, format, and depth, but evidence shows that structured training increases caregiver safety, perceived competence, and reduces stress.
- **Competency checklist:** The KidsVent checklist is the best-known tool. It was built through a Delphi consensus process with 95 Canadian experts, to identify domains and items for a knowledge and skills checklist for caregivers of children requiring tracheostomies with or without ventilation. A 2017 study described its purpose as developing a competency checklist to inform initial training and subsequent assessment of knowledge and skills of family caregivers.
- **Discharge requirements:** A UK-style competency document says caregivers must show competence in the care and replacement of their child's tracheostomy, and that professional caregivers must be present at home on discharge day (Professional caregivers must be available to meet the child at home on the day of discharge.). The same source notes that no consensus exists on how medical stability is defined, though a common approach is no ventilator changes for 1–2 weeks before discharge.
Sources I found don't agree on a standard number of training days. The Riley program is one published example, and the KidsVent checklist is the most-cited competency tool.
