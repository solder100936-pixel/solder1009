## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The 2018 Campbell and Turner review in *Frontiers in Immunology* challenged the "open window" hypothesis, which holds that hard exercise temporarily weakens immunity. The hypothesis says the immune system is compromised in the hours after vigorous exercise, raising the risk of opportunistic infections in the following days. The authors argue the claim rests on thin evidence. They reviewed the key studies and concluded that each of the three pillars supporting the hypothesis has limited evidence behind it. Their broader conclusion is that labeling any form of acute exercise as immunosuppressive is a misconception, and exercise most likely improves immune competency across the lifespan.
The debate is still live. The review itself notes that the hypothesis continues to be discussed despite contradictory evidence. Some of the data it questions is real: a marathon survey found that 12.9% of finishers who were infection-free the week before reported an infection the week after, versus 2.2% of those who withdrew for non-illness reasons. Campbell's explanation is that infections around races may come from crowds rather than exercise itself. He said if people do get an infection, it's probably due to their attendance at a mass-participation event, where lots of people, and their bugs, are gathered. Coverage also notes that the debates won't be resolved without more research.
Press coverage has also gone beyond the paper. One outlet reported the authors' view that infections are more likely to be linked to inadequate diet, stress, and insufficient sleep, which goes further than the review's core claims. The authors later said their work has concluded that there is very limited evidence for exercise directly increasing the risk of becoming infected with viruses.
Overall, the search supports the review's central claim that the evidence for the open window effect is weak, but it does not settle the question. The results didn't include independent critiques of the 2018 paper, so I can't report how the broader field has responded.
