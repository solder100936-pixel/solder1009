## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The Coker 2007 study covered patients with lung disease in general, not COPD alone. It found that 18 percent suffered some type of respiratory symptom on the plane -- most commonly shortness of breath, coughing and chest pain. Those symptoms were usually mild, and there were no serious incidents requiring an emergency landing. The study was published in the European Respiratory Journal in 2007 and is titled "Is air travel safe for those with lung disease?" (RK Coker RJ Shiner MR Partridge. Is air travel safe for those with lung disease? Eur Respir J 2007; 30: 1057–1063.)
Its post-flight data is the part most relevant to your question. Unscheduled respiratory care rose from 9% in the 4 weeks prior to travel to 19% in the 4 weeks after travel. The authors also noted that it's not clear whether those infections were related to air travel.
For COPD specifically, other studies report higher figures, though they vary by recruitment method:
- A Norwegian study of 391 COPD patients found that a majority of COPD patients participating in the study did in fact travel by air (54%) and a minority of these flyers actually experienced dyspnea and air hunger in-flight (28%).
- A separate study of 82 patients with moderate to very severe COPD reported that 38 (46%) patients had experienced respiratory symptoms during air travel.
- A 1991 study of 44 COPD travelers found that eight patients (18.2 %) reported transient symptoms during air travel.
The search did not turn up the exact percentage for COPD patients in the Coker 2007 paper itself, so check the full text if you need that breakdown.
