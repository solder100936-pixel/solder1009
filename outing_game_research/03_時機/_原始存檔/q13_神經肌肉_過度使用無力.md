## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search turned up one main systematic review, plus some background on overtraining risk. Nothing addressed "overwork" in ALS directly, and I didn't find a dedicated review of respiratory muscle fatigue from exercise.
**Main finding:** A 2022 network meta-analysis of 10 RCTs in adults with ALS (Zhu et al., *Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience*) found that physical exercise training showed the highest potential to improve ALS patients' physical function. Its respiratory result was inconclusive: the effect of exercise on the respiratory was still unclear. The authors also said the safety and guide for practice remain unclear, and further high-quality randomized controlled trials (RCTs) with a larger sample are still needed.
**Overwork/safety concern:** The same review notes that the potential negative effects of physical exercise, such as accelerating disease progression or increasing adverse events described in the previous studies, might be related to the form of exercise. It also cites a 2009 study suggesting that the dose-response of physical exercise in healthy individuals appears to be changed in ALS patients, leading them to have a greater risk of overtraining. This is a secondary mention in the review, not its main finding.
**Other reviews cited within it:** A meta-analysis by Rahmati and Malakoutinia found a statistically significant difference in favor of physical exercise in functional ability, overall quality of life, and aerobic capacity for ALS patients, but there was no significant difference in respiratory function, fatigue, pain, and body strength. A 2014 review found limited evidence that inspiratory muscle training leads to the strengthening of inspiratory muscles in ALS.
**Bottom line:** Evidence supports moderate exercise for function and quality of life in ALS, but evidence on respiratory outcomes is unclear, and the overtraining risk is a plausible concern supported mainly by older, limited studies. For specific guidance on respiratory muscle fatigue, a more targeted search on respiratory muscle training and exercise dosing in ALS would be needed.
