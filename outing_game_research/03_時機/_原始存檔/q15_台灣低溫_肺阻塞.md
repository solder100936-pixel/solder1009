## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search didn't turn up a study that matches your exact combination of Taiwan, cold surges, mortality, and lag structure. It did surface several related findings, mostly from other settings, and two that cite Taiwan.
- **Taiwan case-crossover study:** A Frontiers paper reports, via a cited Taiwan study, that the exacerbation rate of COPD on event days increased by 0.8% for every 1°C decrease in air temperature (95% C.I. 1.015–1.138). This is a secondhand citation, so I'd check the original Taiwan paper before relying on it.
- **Taiwan 2000–2008 analysis:** A Dovepress article notes that research conducted in Taiwan from 2000 to 2008 has reported a U-shaped association between temperature and COPD outcomes. That means risk rises at both temperature extremes, not only in the cold.
- **Lag structure elsewhere:** A Panzhihua, China study using a distributed lag non-linear model found that the lag effects of low temperature and relatively low temperature peaked at 2th day, with the lag effects disappearing at 16–17 days. It also concluded that low temperatures are associated with an increased risk of AECOPD hospitalizations.
- **Beijing study:** A Beijing time-series analysis of 143,318 admissions found that lower temperature is associated with a higher risk for AECOPD hospitalizations. It also reported a multi-week cumulative effect; one Frontiers summary notes a "U-shaped" association between DTDmean and 21 days cumulative relative risk of AECOPD.
For the Taiwan-specific mortality and cold-surge lag question, the original Taiwan papers (including the case-crossover study and the 2000–2008 analysis) are the next places to look.
