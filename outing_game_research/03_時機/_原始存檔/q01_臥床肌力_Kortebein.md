## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search turned up the original Kortebein studies, but no source gives a clean per-day strength loss rate, so any per-day figure has to be my own arithmetic.
- **Lean mass (2007 JAMA report):** Healthy older adults lost almost 1 kg of lower-extremity lean tissue over 10 days of bed rest, with a 16% drop in isokinetic knee extensor strength (Recently, we reported that healthy older adults lose almost 1 kg of lean tissue from the lower extremities after 10 days of bed rest, with an associated 16% decline in isokinetic knee extensor strength). That works out to roughly 100 g of leg lean mass per day.
- **Strength (2008 J Gerontol follow-up):** The 11 participants (mean age 67) were on continuous bed rest with a diet meeting the protein recommendation. Their isotonic knee extensor strength (-13.2 ± 4.1%, p = .004) and stair-climbing power (-14 ± 4.1%, p = .01) both declined. The 2008 paper also reports that 10 days of bed rest results in a substantial loss of lower extremity strength, power, and aerobic capacity, and a reduction in physical activity, but has no effect on physical performance.
- **Comparison with younger adults:** The muscle and strength losses in older adults were greater than that observed in young adults after 14 or 28 days of bed rest.
- **Caveat on the numbers:** The 16% (isokinetic) and 13.2% (isotonic) strength figures come from different measurement types, so they aren't directly interchangeable. Dividing by 10 gives roughly 1.3–1.6% per day, but losses are unlikely to be linear, so treat that as a rough average.
