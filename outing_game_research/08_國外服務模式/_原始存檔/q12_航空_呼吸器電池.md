## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
I found no UK CAA primary source in the results, so this is based on airline pages and a few secondary guides. The CAA's own rules, and the CAA's 100–160Wh position, still need checking directly on caa.co.uk.

**Lithium batteries (100Wh/160Wh thresholds)**
- Qantas lists lithium-ion batteries up to 100Wh in medical equipment as not needing airline approval, allowed in carry-on or checked bags. Batteries above 100Wh up to 160Wh need airline approval and must be declared at check-in. Lithium-ion batteries (rechargeable) - not more than 100Wh in medical equipment and the 100–160Wh tier is listed separately as needing approval.
- British Airways has a form for requesting approval of spare lithium batteries between 100Wh and 160Wh, for example those used in Portable Electronic Devices (PEDs), like video or portable medical equipment. If your battery(ies) are found to be acceptable for carriage, the maximum number permitted is two. This form is on BA's Canadian, Australian and Turkish sites, so check the UK version.
- Swiss rules for spares above 100Wh: the capacity of a single battery must not exceed 160 Wh, a maximum of two spare batteries is permitted, and they must be carried in your carry-on baggage. Battery packs are assessed by their combined Wh total.
- Cathay Pacific forbids equipment and spares over 160Wh (8g) on passenger flights, and it allows spare lithium batteries only in carry-on baggage.

**Ventilators and CPAP**
- Cathay Pacific asks for at least 48 hours' notice to use a ventilator or CPAP in flight. Passengers who only require the use of a CPAP or BIPAP machine inflight do not need medical clearance.
- Swiss requires technical authorisation for CPAP and says bringing a medical certificate in English confirming the need to use the CPAP or VIPAP device on board helps at security. Its ventilator and POC form says the use of a POC or BIPAP device requires medical and technical authorisation from SWISS.
- easyJet asks for contact at least 48 hours before departure, and be sure your equipment is battery powered.
- Airlines differ. One guide says some airlines like Ryanair, for example, will not allow you to use CPAPs during the flight.

**Practical points**
- Notify the airline in advance. One UK guide says these rules require airlines to allow passengers with essential medical equipment to travel with them without incurring extra fees, provided the passenger notifies the airline 48 hours before their trip. That claim is not verified against a CAA source here.
- Qantas says equipment containing batteries must have the ON/OFF switch protected to prevent accidental activation when packed in checked baggage, and the device must be fully off.
- Check the rules for your departure, arrival and connecting airports, since special requirements may apply.
- A US-focused guide puts spares in carry-on only and notes that up to two larger spares (101 to 160 Wh) allowed with airline approval. This is FAA rather than UK guidance, so use it only as background.

**Gaps:** Nothing above confirms the current CAA rules directly. The airadvisor UK guide cites a regulation number and amendment that I could not verify. Confirm with the CAA, the airline's special assistance team, and the device maker's battery documentation.
