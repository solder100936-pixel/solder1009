## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The regulation says a transit provider may not bar a rider with a disability from traveling with a respirator or portable oxygen supply, but that protection is limited by federal hazmat rules. The search returned only the opening clause of 37.167(h), so I couldn't confirm the rest of its text. The provision is tied to applicable Department of Transportation rules on hazardous materials transportation, and that tie is what agencies rely on when they write their own policies.

Local transit policies commonly add conditions. One agency's policy says riders may bring respirators and properly secured portable oxygen supplies, and oxygen supplies must not obstruct the aisle. Another adds that this is allowed as long as it does not violate laws or rules related to transportation of hazardous materials.

The equipment type matters. A San Luis Obispo transit guidance letter treats a portable oxygen concentrator as distinct from a cylinder, noting your oxygen supply, however, is not a "cylinder" but a portable oxygen concentrator. Compressed oxygen is a separate matter: compressed oxygen is regulated as a Hazardous Material by the Pipeline and Hazardous Materials Safety Administration under title 49 CFR 172.101.

Air travel follows different rules. Aviation oxygen carriage is governed by 14 CFR 121.574, not by the ADA paratransit provisions.
