## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search didn't return the operative text of 14 CFR 382.133, so I can't confirm the 150 percent battery rule, the 48-hour notice requirement, or the ventilator carrier provisions from these results.

What the results do show:

- **The 2016 final rule amended § 382.133.** It took effect August 22, 2016, and replaced the case-by-case POC approval process with acceptance criteria, removing SFAR 106 (amendments to 14 CFR 11.201, 121.306, 125.204, 135.144, 382,27, and 382.133, and the removal of Special Federal Aviation Regulation No. 106 are effective August 22, 2016.).
- **Battery rules come from the acceptance criteria and the hazmat rules.** The rule says the POC must not contain any hazardous materials subject to the HMR, except as provided for batteries in the exceptions for crewmembers and passengers (49 CFR 175.10).
- **Older SFAR 106 language is related but superseded.** It required batteries to be protected from short circuit and covered a flight-duration basis for battery counts, including a conservative estimate of any unanticipated delays. This is the predecessor framework, not the current § 382.133 text, so the 150 percent figure may come from there or from the current rule. I couldn't verify which.
- **Some results are off-target.** The 2011 CFR excerpt concerns foreign carriers and medical portable electronic devices, not battery counts.

To verify the current figures, check the eCFR version of 14 CFR 382.133 directly, and the FAA's current POC acceptance criteria and AC 120-95A for battery and notice requirements. The ventilator and respiratory assistive device carrier provisions were not addressed in these results.
