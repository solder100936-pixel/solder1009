## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search didn't turn up anything that directly covers revealing hidden choices or missed opportunities in an end-of-game summary. The results are about debriefing more generally.

- **Reflection as a design object:** One recent VR serious-game paper argues that debriefs can expose the design's own framing, such as what a scenario simplified or left out, so that design choices become things learners can critique. Asking why a relative could plead but not receive an explanation turns design choices into discussable objects. It also notes that debriefing cannot retroactively supply an in-play affordance, so it can only make restrictions visible, not fix them.
- **Purpose of reflection:** A reference document on serious game design describes reflection as giving players a chance to analyze their actions and consider how these choices relate to their own lives. It suggests formats ranging from a timeline snapshot of the player's activities to a character prompting the player to recount their experience.
- **Automated self-debriefing:** One study built a self-debriefing system for a cyber-bullying game with multiple paths to a solution. It combined text feedback with three visualizations and found the debriefing did help the participants in having a better understanding of the outcome of the game. This is the closest match to showing alternative paths, though the paper's focus is on visualization comparison.
- **Pitfalls:** A TU Delft study of debriefing pitfalls found that all 12 pitfalls seem to occur in practice, to some extent, due to both the design and the execution of the debriefing.

For sources specifically on showing unchosen paths or counterfactual outcomes, a more targeted search would be needed.
