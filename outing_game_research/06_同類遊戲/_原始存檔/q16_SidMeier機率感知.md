## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search turned up good coverage of Meier's 2010 GDC talk, "The Psychology of Game Design (Everything You Know Is Wrong)," though the odds in the sources don't match your 2/3 figure.

- **Combat odds:** Several write-ups describe the same pattern. Meier's team found players expected to win fights with about a 75% chance, and players still felt they could win at 1:3 odds. One report says when a player faces an opponent, and has 3:1 odds, the player should lose one out of four times. The game was then made to cheat a bit behind the scenes, to make 3:1 stronger than it seemed.
- **Adjusting the math:** One account says Meier had to make the odds even odder in many cases to adjust for players' expectation of how "fair" a game feels. Another says he changed the math again so that the outcomes of previous battles are taken into account. The reason given was that if a player lost too many 2-to-1 battles in a row, they would get frustrated.
- **Broader point:** Meier argued that player psychology has nothing to do with rational thought. He also said any kind of randomness needs to be treated with a lot of care.

Your query's "2/3 odds" doesn't match what the sources report. The examples cited are 3:1 and 1.5 vs 0.5 (both about a 75% win chance), not 2/3. Where the sources differ, the 3:1 example is the most consistently reported. The GDC Vault page for the talk is members-only, so I couldn't check the slides or the original recording.
