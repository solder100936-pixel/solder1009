## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search turned up several reviews. The most directly relevant is a 2024 meta-analysis of undergraduate nursing students, but its reported numbers are confusing, so treat its direction of effect with caution.

- **Nursing-student meta-analysis (2024, *Nurse Education in Practice*):** This review pooled randomized and quasi-experimental studies from February–March 2023. It concluded that aged simulation interventions can be a useful way to help students empathize with an older adult. The attitude results were weak, since one pooled estimate found the intervention group's attitudes were similar to those of the control group. The empathy results read as if the control group scored higher, which conflicts with the conclusion, so the sign may be reversed in the reporting. I'd check the full text before relying on that direction.
- **Gerhardy et al. (2022, *European Journal of Ageing*):** This systematic review covered 26 studies, and study populations were predominantly students in health-related disciplines. It reported that suits can have positive effects on attitudes toward older adults (d = 0.33) and on empathy for older adults (d = 0.54). However, physical performance was significantly reduced, but there is little evidence that the simulation realistically reflects typical aging. The authors also said more diverse study populations and high-quality controlled designs are needed.
- **Eost-Telling et al. (2021, *Journal of Advanced Nursing*):** This mixed-methods review of 23 studies found that simulations of ageing increased empathy towards older adults, but they also increased feelings of anxiety about ageing. A separate summary noted that two studies found the positive effects dropped over time, which suggests repeated exposure may help reinforce attitude change.

Overall, the evidence suggests suits can raise empathy among health-related students, with weaker and less consistent effects on attitudes. The studies are often small and low in quality, and they tend to test whether suits feel like aging rather than whether they accurately reproduce it.
