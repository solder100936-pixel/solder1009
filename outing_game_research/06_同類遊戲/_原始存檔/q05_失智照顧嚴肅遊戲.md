I ran one search. None of the results mention the specific titles you listed ("Before I Forget," "The Caregiver," or "Spent"), so I can't speak to those games. The results cover related work.

**Caregiver-focused design and metrics:** Liu and Wills (2022) argue that dementia family carers get little support and that games could help meet their needs. They identified three carer needs (health, education, social) and built a metric instrument on the Goal Question Metric method. In a small exploratory test with six health games and five game professionals, the instrument could tell how well games met those needs, though the authors say a larger study focused on one game is still needed. (An exploratory experiment was conducted with six health-related games and five game professionals to assess the effectiveness of the instrument.)

**Games played by people with dementia, with empathy as an outcome:** Berenbaum et al. (2020) tested a tablet game co-designed with people with dementia. In a 12-week proof-of-concept study with 27 participants, observers rated emotional and social intelligence during sessions. The authors report that the game evoked responses related to self-awareness, empathy, and communication, and they suggest it may help bridge the gap between carer and person with dementia. The sample was small, and the authors call for larger studies. (Despite declines in cognitive abilities, our study shows that a tablet based SG4D can stimulate their ESI and evoke responses in self-awareness, empathy, social and communication capacities.)

**Gap in the literature:** Berenbaum et al. note that most research on dementia targets cognitive decline and overlooks the emotional capacities that remain. (Unfortunately, most efforts related to PwD focus on cognitive changes from dementia while ignoring the fact that there is remaining emotional intelligence.)

**Related work:** The COSMA framework targets people with early dementia and uses remotely activated family content to trigger memories. Its authors report a small but significant rise in positive emotion after play. (Our findings demonstrated a small, but significant increase in positive emotions)
