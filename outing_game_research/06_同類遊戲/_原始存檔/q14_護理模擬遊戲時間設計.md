## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
I found no study that directly compares real-time and turn-based versions of a nursing serious game. The closest work is a randomized trial of a turn-based-style game against paper cases, plus a few smaller studies on debriefing and time pressure.

- **Turn-based-style design:** The LabForGames Warning game is not played in real time, but it shows a clock so players feel time passing. The game is not played in real time but a clock was presented on the screen which indicated the time flow.
- **Randomized trial against traditional teaching:** Second-year nursing students were split between the game and paper cases with a teacher review. In the serious game arm, the student played individually with a serious game consisting of 2 cases followed by a common debriefing with an instructor. The trial found no difference: no significant educational difference (SCT), neither immediate nor 1 month later, was observed between training by SG and the TT course.
- **Debriefing:** Instructor-led debriefing has clear benefits in high-fidelity simulation, but its role with serious games is less settled. Its place is unclear regarding the use of a serious game. In the LabForGames setup, the game also gave on-screen feedback after each step, while a common interactive debriefing is provided by an instructor at the end of each scenario.
- **Qualitative clinical reasoning:** A Western University thesis on a serious-gaming digital simulation found that reflective debriefing post-simulation may increase learning and improvement.
- **Time-pressured format:** A Suffolk pilot gamified a paper-based decision-making session built around a sequence of four time-based scenarios. It was only tested with faculty, so no outcome data are available.

The evidence is thin. The one randomized trial compared a game against paper and did not test real-time versus turn-based play. Searching for "real-time" or "timed" serious games in nursing with an outcome measure, or for virtual patient comparisons, would be the next step.
