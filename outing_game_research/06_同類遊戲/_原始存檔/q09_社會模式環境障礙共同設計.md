## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search turned up related research but nothing that combines the social model, route-planning barriers, and wheelchair navigation in one game design study.

- **Co-design evidence:** A 2016 study compared wheelchair-using young people with game design students. Both groups' concepts were generally suitable examples of wheelchair-controlled motion-based games, though they differed in level of detail and in their ideas of disability. A separate paper found that involving young people who use wheelchairs and game design experts produced comparable game concepts, though the authors caution that participation should be adjusted to individual abilities to avoid frustration.
- **Social model in games:** One source describes the social model as the approach most often used in accessibility work, where a person's limitations are determined by their environment rather than by their condition. A 2008 paper on the social construction model of interactive gaming for disabled users also exists, though its snippet is abstract and mostly about self-identity and social effects. It notes there has been little research on how the social construction model applies to interactive games.
- **Environmental barriers and co-creation:** A 2023 study of environmental games found these games lack a specific focus on accessibility, and its authors argue that accessibility should be co-created with users and implemented from the design stage.
- **Wheelchair-specific design:** A recent paper on wheelchair users' perspectives on movement-based games reports themes around independent access, challenges in social settings, and the need for better design support, and notes that existing design guidelines overwhelmingly focus on non-disabled players.

Gaps: I did not find a source on route planning or pathfinding as an accessibility barrier in wheelchair-based games specifically. A follow-up search on accessible navigation or pathfinding in games, or on "wheelchair user pathfinding game" research, would be the next step if you need that.
