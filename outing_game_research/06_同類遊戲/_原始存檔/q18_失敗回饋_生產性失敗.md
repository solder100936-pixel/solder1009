## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search turned up several studies on failure feedback in educational games, though most are abstracts or listings rather than full papers.

- **Scoring and error-avoidance:** Westera (2022) argues that performance-based scoring pushes players toward avoiding errors, which removes chances to learn from mistakes. His computational model suggests performance metrics overestimate learning early in a game and underestimate it later. A catalog summary recommends that scoring systems avoid penalizing mistakes, reward error correction, and give enough feedback on failure.
- **Metacognitive response to failure:** Lee's doctoral work (Columbia) found that failing in an electrical circuit puzzle game before formal instruction led to deeper learning, and one strategy, "information-seeking and fixing," predicted later performance. A second study added prompts to the game to encourage that strategy.
- **Failure and discourse:** Anderson et al. (2018) studied 88 middle schoolers playing an educational virology game. A higher number of failures before initial success at levels predicted greater learning gains than time on task. The authors tied this to group discussion after failures, which they interpreted through the productive failure framework.
- **Process vs. result feedback:** A master's thesis (Luttikhold, 2018) tested process-oriented and result-oriented feedback in a math game. It found no differences in perseverance, but process-oriented feedback appeared to affect how students responded to a failure experience. Its sample was small and the evidence was tentative.
- **Extra lives:** A 2026 quasi-experimental geography game study (Gould et al.) found that participants reported more frustration when given fewer chances, especially those less familiar with games.
- **Randomized trial:** Gauthier and Jenkinson (2018) tested how game design can promote learning from failure in biology misconceptions. Their analysis of gameplay video offers design recommendations.

Overall, the evidence supports failure as a learning mechanism when feedback helps learners diagnose and fix errors, but most studies are small, and the full texts weren't available here. The Filament Games blog and the UniversityXP pages are secondary commentary, so I'd rely on the primary studies above.
