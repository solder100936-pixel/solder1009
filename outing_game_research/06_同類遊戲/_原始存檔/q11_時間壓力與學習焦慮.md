## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
I didn't find a study that directly compares timed and untimed serious games while measuring both anxiety and learning. The closest evidence comes from adjacent research, and the findings are mixed.

- **Arithmetic practice:** In a study of adults learning times tables, time pressure during learning elevated participants' perceived stress. It also led to faster response times during testing when learning was spaced daily and every other day, but slower response times for all other spacings. Timed and untimed practice led to similar forgetting of practiced facts over time, but untimed practice allowed participants to gradually improve on unpracticed facts and conceptually related facts.
- **Stress and cognition:** A review-based paper notes that time pressure can enhance cognitive load and induce stress, potentially hindering performance and learning.
- **Engineering simulation:** A construction simulator study found no significant difference in the learning process, but increased time pressure resulted in more unacceptable answers, and in increased motivation to score high, which students tried to reach through a "trial and error" mentality. The authors recommended caution regarding the specific amount of time pressure.
- **Crisis management game:** Researchers predicted the experimental condition (time pressure vs. no time pressure) with 84.5% accuracy, using a game-only feature set. This shows time pressure changes player behavior, but it doesn't measure learning outcomes.
- **Practitioner view:** An eLearning industry article reports that some learners shut down under time pressure, even if they know the answer, and it recommends matching the timer to real-world time demands.

A 2023 paper on math anxiety and perfectionism in timed versus untimed testing with gifted sixth graders appears in a reference list, but I could not retrieve its findings. Searching for that title or for "timed untimed serious game anxiety" in journal databases would be the next step.
