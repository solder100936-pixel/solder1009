## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
I found several related games, but none clearly matches your description of an educational game where players uncover hidden eligibility rules.

- **The Determinator** is the closest match. It's a desk simulation where you process disability, retirement, and supplemental income claims by checking each file against a manual and choosing to approve, deny, or request more information. Additional cases are generated each day, all held to the same rules and the same manual. It's a narrative game with moral choices and multiple endings, not an explicit educational tool, and I didn't find evidence that it's designed around players discovering hidden rules.
- **Papers, Please-style sims** like this one are a recurring genre. A similar title, a dystopian sim about denying health insurance claims, has a medical procedure that was fully covered on Monday might be an immediate denial by Wednesday. That suggests shifting rules as a mechanic, though it's framed around private insurance rather than public benefits.
- **ADA Game**, listed on the MERLOT educational resource site, simulates how advocacy can promote positive changes. It's the most explicitly educational result, but the listing gives few details on its mechanics.

For research context, a 2021 dissertation examines how SSI and SSDI recipients describe their dealings with the Social Security Administration as a "game" in which they strive to meet their basic needs although the agency seeks to minimize benefits. The study is about lived experience rather than a game, but it may help frame an educational design.

If you're looking for a game where discovering hidden rules is the core learning mechanic, I didn't find a clear match in this search. A follow-up search on specific titles or on "Rules as Code" educational games would be the next step.
