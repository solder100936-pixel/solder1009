## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
I ran one search. It found research on older adults' game preferences and design, but nothing that directly tests turn-based versus real-time design or gives specific seniors' accessibility guidelines.

**Preferences and time pressure**
- A research pilot survey lists difficulty making quick decisions under time pressure as a cognitive challenge older gamers report. CH7 Cognitive Challenges Difficulty making quick decisions under time pressure Time Pressure [26, 33]
- A review of older adults' game preferences found they lean toward casual games and educational games over more immersive and realistic 3D games (action games, fighting games, first-person shooters, real time strategy games). The authors link this to a preference for intellectually stimulating games over those that reward fast reactions.
- Older adults have said some games are simply too challenging. Older adults expressed the ideas that video games are too challenging, and that in general they are a waste of time.

**Difficulty and calibration**
- One review notes that games that are well-calibrated to players can be a barrier to adoption. The surrounding text is about players' abilities, so it is a general point rather than a finding about turn-based design specifically.
- Preferences vary widely. Research has found older adults are not a homogenous group in terms of preferences. Any guidance should therefore offer options rather than a single "senior mode."

**Design guidance**
- Most games are built for younger players. Despite this growth, most games are still designed with younger players in mind.
- The main design recommendation in the literature is to conform to principles of good design for older adults and engage them directly in the design process.

**Caveats:** The Steam and Lemmy forum results in the search were anecdotal opinions about turn-based games and age, so I did not rely on them. The sources above are mostly from one research group (CREATE) and a 2020 review, so the evidence base is limited. For specific guidelines (for example, adjustable time limits or pause options), I would need a follow-up search on game accessibility standards or the Game Accessibility Guidelines.
