## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
I ran one search. It turned up useful options for alerting caregivers using eye movement, a switch, or a head-mounted device, but nothing on the ventilator, staffing, or home-safety side of your question.

- **Eye-triggered emergency alerts:** The EyeControl wearable can trigger an emergency call when the user blinks three times. A separate low-cost prototype, the ALS Alert System, uses eye-movement sensors to trigger a loud alarm to alert caregivers nearby and can also send alerts to a phone app using cloud communication. A Drexel project describes a similar goal: giving people in advanced stages of ALS lead to LiS a way to alert caregivers, with vertical eye movements as the trigger.
- **Switch-adapted call button:** One caregiver's account describes a person with ALS who bumps a switch adapted call button with his head to call for help when the caregiver isn't nearby. The caregiver also uses a baby monitor to listen in.
- **Backup communication:** When eye gaze isn't practical, a caregiver describes using a letter board and yes/no questions. Limits include bright light, which causes reflections that interfere with the camera, and driving a wheelchair. Yes/no questions are our only option in the dark.

The sources are mostly student projects, company pages, and caregiver blogs, not clinical guidance. Before relying on any device, check its availability and reliability with the ALS clinic or your care team. Questions about ventilator-dependent care, staying safe outside the home, and never being left alone would need a separate search.
