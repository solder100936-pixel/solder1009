## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search turned up useful background on heat and autonomic risks, but no practical travel checklist. Nothing on ventilator logistics for travel came up.

**Thermoregulation:** Injuries above T6 disrupt the nervous system's ability to control heat loss and heat conservation, which leaves people vulnerable to both overheating and chilling. One study found that people with tetraplegia are at higher risk than those with paraplegia, and that warm seasonal temperatures hurt their comfort and activity participation more than they affect people without SCI. Some people with tetraplegia also report needing 20 minutes or more to cool down after overheating. A clinically recognized but little-studied pattern called "quad fever," with high fevers and no real rise in core temperature, is also described. For travel, this suggests planning for climate control, avoiding prolonged heat exposure, and carrying layers for cold environments.

**Autonomic dysreflexia:** This is a sudden, dangerous blood pressure spike triggered by stimuli below the level of injury, and it can escalate to severe hypertension. Common triggers include anything painful or uncomfortable that the person may not feel, such as a full bladder, bowel problems, or pressure injuries. Travelers should know how to check blood pressure, carry any emergency protocol their care team has given them, and have a companion who can help.

**Ventilator and breathing:** One source notes that people with injuries above C4 may have a weak diaphragm and need a ventilator, and that people with injuries at C5 and below often need help coughing. Other sources note that pulmonary complications are a major cause of illness and death in tetraplegia. I didn't find specific advice on ventilator power, backup equipment, or airline and hotel accommodations.

**Gaps:** I didn't find a travel-specific guideline. For a tailored plan, a spinal cord injury clinic or the person's physiatrist or pulmonologist is the best source. The ISAFSCI autonomic standards and the International SCI thermoregulation data set are also useful references for documenting baseline function.
