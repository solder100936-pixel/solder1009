## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search didn't turn up the 2022 BTS statement itself. The results mostly cover the 2011 BTS guidance and related studies, so I can't confirm what the 2022 version says about these points.

- **Vital capacity threshold:** The 2011 BTS guidance lists severe restrictive disease with vital capacity under 1 litre, including chest wall and respiratory muscle disease, as a group needing extra attention, especially with hypoxaemia and/or hypercapnia.
- **Hypoxic challenge test:** For neuromuscular disease and kyphoscoliosis, the 2011 guidance leaves the decision on in-flight oxygen and/or non-invasive ventilation to individual clinical judgement. It also states that all patients with severe extrapulmonary restriction, including those needing home ventilation, should undergo HCT before travel.
- **Evidence base:** A 2009 Thorax study of 19 patients with kyphoscoliosis or neuromuscular disease found that 15 of 19 met the criteria suggested by the BTS Standards of Care Committee for in-flight oxygen regardless of baseline SaO2. The authors noted that the BTS recommendation was based on very few data.
- **NIV during flight:** A study from Sorbonne University (a preprint, so treat it as provisional) describes NIV as a new alternative to oxygen therapy for neuromuscular patients planning to take a flight.
- **Test duration:** A Toronto cross-sectional study compared the standard 20-minute HCT with a 120-minute version to see whether the longer test identified more individuals with neuromuscular disease at potential risk.

To check the 2022 statement's exact thresholds for NIV and HCT, go to the BTS website directly.
