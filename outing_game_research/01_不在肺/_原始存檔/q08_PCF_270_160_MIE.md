## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search turned up the thresholds and the AARC recommendation, though the sources are secondary and somewhat mixed.

- **Thresholds:** One source describes peak cough flow under 160 L/min as indicating an ineffective cough, 161–270 L/min as impaired, and above 270 L/min as effective ([HRA research summary]). A review of mechanical insufflation-exsufflation (MI-E) notes that Bach et al. found about 270 L/min was needed to avoid respiratory failure in Duchenne patients during respiratory infections ([Mechanical Insufflation-Exsufflation review, Scribd copy]).
- **Guideline:** A systematic review in *Respiratory Care* (Auger et al., 2017) reports that the American Association for Respiratory Care recommended cough-assist techniques for neuromuscular patients, particularly when peak flow is at or below 270 L/min, since it can drop below 160 L/min during respiratory infection ([Respiratory Care, 2017]).
- **Caveat:** The same review concludes that current evidence does not support MI-E for cough augmentation in neuromuscular disease, so the guideline's strength is contested ([Respiratory Care, 2017]).
- **Other guidance:** A 2009 review states that patients with unassisted peak cough flow under 160 L/min will require mechanical insufflation/exsufflation ([Chatwin, 2009]). A 2019 paper also treats 160 L/min as the criterion for starting MI-E ([Arch Bronconeumol, 2019]).

I didn't find the primary AARC guideline text itself, so verify the exact wording and the 270 L/min cutoff against that document before citing it.
