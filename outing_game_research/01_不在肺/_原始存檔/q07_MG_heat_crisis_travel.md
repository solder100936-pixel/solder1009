## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search turned up useful material on heat, crisis warning signs, and travel planning, but little on medication timing.

**Heat:** Heat is a recognized trigger. The Myasthenia Gravis Foundation lists heat among triggers that can worsen weakness, alongside infection, stress from trauma or surgery, and stopping or reducing MG medications (Triggers which may worsen MG muscle weakness include: botulinum toxin, stopping or reducing medications used to treat MG, illness or infection, heat, stress from trauma or surgery). Myasthenia Gravis News notes that heat-induced myasthenic crises are rare but serious and require emergency care. Its cooling guidance is in a separate article.

**Warning signs:** Early crisis signs are often subtle. One source says early crisis signs are usually bulbar rather than dramatic: coughing on liquids, pooling saliva, a nasal or fading voice, and a head that drops forward. The Foundation adds that severe slurred speech and difficulty managing secretions are also signs of potential impending MG crisis. Myasthenia Gravis News advises seeking immediate care for increased difficulty breathing, swallowing, or speaking. A separate source advises calling emergency services if breathing feels effortful, you cannot clear your throat, or you feel drowsy or confused.

**Travel:** The Foundation suggests discussing travel plans with your physician and asking for a list of MG-experienced providers near your destination (Make sure to discuss your plans for travel with your physician, as they can help you weigh the risks of travel. You can also ask your doctor for a list of recommended MG providers near your destination.). Other advice includes carrying an emergency card or alert bracelet (Carry an emergency card in your wallet or wear an alert bracelet identifying you as a person with myasthenia gravis.) and choosing refundable plans with travel insurance.

**Medication timing:** None of the results gave specific guidance on dosing schedules across time zones or on managing medication during travel. Ask your neurologist for that, and bring a written medication schedule and a supply that covers any delays.
