## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search returned strong guidance on the emergency card and anesthesia/oxygen precautions, but nothing specific to air travel with a ventilator or cough assist.

**Oxygen:** PPMD's guidance is to give oxygen only with close CO2 monitoring, since breathing may need support such as BiPAP. If oxygen levels are low, assisted coughing (with cough assist machine or Ambu bag) may help. Its emergency card says do not give oxygen without close monitoring, and its newer materials note that many emergency personnel will default to administering oxygen during respiratory distress, which is a risk for people with Duchenne.

**Anesthesia:** Inhaled anesthetics are to be avoided where possible. PPMD's provider sheet says inhaled anesthesia can cause rhabdomyolysis among other serious complications. IV anesthesia is generally considered safe with close monitoring, and succinylcholine should NEVER be used. Local anesthesia and nitrous oxide are safe for most patients with Duchenne, especially patients who are ambulatory with normal pulmonary function.

**Travel and equipment:** PPMD advises that you take your equipment with you to the hospital/emergency room (ER); alert your neuromuscular team that you are going to the ER/hospital. The MDA's alert card includes fields for whether the person uses BiPAP at night, and its provider section advises extreme caution is recommended if oxygen becomes necessary.

**Gaps:** None of the results addressed airline rules, oxygen or pressurization in cabins, or how to pack and power a ventilator for a flight. For that, you would need to check with the airline and the neuromuscular team or a respiratory therapist.
