## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The main evidence is a 2024 single-centre retrospective audit, and it supports your concern. Normal oxygen saturation does not rule out CO2 retention in neuromuscular disease (NMD).

- **The guideline threshold:** NICE's motor neurone disease guidance suggests an arterial blood gas when SpO2 is 94% or lower and there is no lung disease. The basis of this guideline is that normoxia (SpO2 ≥95%) usually results in an arterial partial pressure of carbon dioxide (PaCO2) ≤45 mmHg, which assumes a respiratory quotient of 0.8.
- **The challenge to that threshold:** In Gray et al. (ERJ Open Research, 2024), none of the patients without NMD showed hypercapnia with normoxia. In contrast, 54% of patients with other NMD and 36% of motor neuron disease patients displayed this condition.
- **Proposed mechanism:** The authors suggest a higher calculated respiratory quotient in NMD could explain this. They conclude that hypercapnia cannot be ruled out by normal oximetry in patients with NMD.
- **Oxygen caution:** An AMDA document on Pompe disease notes that supplemental oxygen alone, without assisted ventilation, can raise oxygen levels but reduce the brain's drive to breathe, allowing CO₂ to accumulate further. It recommends that assisted ventilation should be prioritized before adding oxygen, with monitoring by capnography or blood gas.

Caveats: the Gray study is retrospective, single-centre, and based on room-air blood gases from patients with chronic hypercapnic respiratory failure. The AMDA document is patient-advocacy guidance rather than a formal guideline, and I did not find a formal guideline that explicitly changes the SpO2 threshold for NMD.
