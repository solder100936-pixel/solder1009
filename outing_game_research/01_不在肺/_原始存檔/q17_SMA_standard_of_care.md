The search turned up the main guideline documents, but not the specific details you listed (oximetry-guided cough assist, seating, or brace effects on breathing).

- **Core guidelines:** The 2007 consensus statement was the first standard-of-care document for SMA. The 2018 Part 2 update revisits pulmonary management and acute care, along with newer topics such as other organ involvement and medications. Those 2018 recommendations appear in the Neuromuscular Disorders journal.
- **Acute illness planning:** The family guide to the consensus advises planning for times when a child is ill, with a care plan for colds, flu, or similar illnesses. It also notes that muscle weakness can make a child more susceptible to respiratory infections.
- **Cough assist:** A 2020 review recommends training patients and caregivers in cough-assisted techniques when respiratory muscle strength falls below 50% of predicted or with recurrent or severe respiratory infections.
- **Monitoring:** The same review says regular monitoring of respiratory muscle performance helps anticipate complications such as acute and chronic respiratory failure.
- **Posture and chest wall:** The 2018 Part 2 document describes non-sitter management aimed at preparing for respiratory failure and preventing chest wall distortion (Finkel et al.), but the search snippet did not include its specifics on seating, posture, or bracing.
- **International standards:** The UK SMA organization maintains updated international standards with a chapter on breathing (respiratory and pulmonary care) and a separate chapter on orthopaedic management, which would cover scoliosis bracing.
