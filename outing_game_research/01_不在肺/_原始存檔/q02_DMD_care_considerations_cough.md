The search confirmed the 2018 respiratory guidance but did not surface the specific thresholds, travel guidance, or emergency card details you asked about.

- **2018 update:** The DMD Care Considerations were updated in 2018 to emphasize multidisciplinary care and earlier lung function testing. Lung function testing should also include maximum inspiratory pressure (PImax), maximum expiratory pressure (PEmax), cough peak flow (CPF), and capnography.
- **Thresholds:** The 2018 respiratory part endorses higher pulmonary function thresholds (ie, milder levels of respiratory impairment) for initiation of assisted coughing and assisted ventilation than were recommended in the 2010 care considerations. The search did not give the actual cut-off values, so I can't confirm the 270 or 160 figures from these results.
- **The 270 figure:** One study used an unassisted cough peak flow <270 L/min as an eligibility cutoff for enrollment. That is a research inclusion criterion, not a clinical threshold for starting care. Parents and caregivers and health-care providers were ineffective at increasing cough peak flow in children with neuromuscular weakness when applying a manually assisted cough.
- **Anticipatory care:** A patient-advocacy summary of the 2018 guidance says even if respiratory function is adequate, your pulmonologist may still want you or your child to start using assistive equipment.
- **Cough and monitoring:** Weak coughing muscles can put people with Duchenne at risk for mucus plugging, atelectasis (section of the lung collapsing), and pneumonia. A 2025 review lists decreased vital capacity, maximal inspiratory and expiratory pressures, and peak cough flow as key monitoring parameters.
- **Gaps:** Nothing in these results addressed travel planning, oxygen use, or emergency cards. Those would need a separate search, or the 2018 Care Considerations article itself.
