Children with CCHS are able to lead active lives but need close supervision while swimming or playing in pools. Hopkins also says the condition is lifelong but that proper treatment does not shorten life expectancy (CCHS is a lifelong condition, but with proper treatment it does not alter life expectancy.).
- **Missing breathlessness signal:** A reprint of the ATS policy statement notes that affected people may be unusually good at underwater swimming or breath-holding because they cannot perceive the sensation of suffocation (index 2-2). It also says they generally need careful medical supervision to reach a good quality of life (index 2-4). This is a secondhand reprint, so check the ATS original before relying on exact wording.
- **Clinical reference:** A PubMed-indexed clinical summary lists swimming and breath-holding contests as circumstances to avoid, because of the risk of asphyxia and death (index 9-7).
