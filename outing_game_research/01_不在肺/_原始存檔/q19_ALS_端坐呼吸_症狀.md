## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search didn't turn up the NICE MND guideline (NG42) text itself, so I couldn't confirm its respiratory criteria. What I did find:

- **Supine FVC drop:** A health-outcomes study on dyspnea in ALS says that a decline up to 10% can be considered normal and in case of ΔFVC > 25% in conjunction with restrictive pulmonary function relevant diaphragmatic weakness can be assumed. This is a single journal article, not a guideline.
- **Indications for ventilation support:** An ALS.org slide deck, which cites AAN-based guidance, lists orthopnea, sniff nasal pressure below 40 cm H2O, maximal inspiratory pressure above -60 cmH2O, abnormal sleep oximetry, and Forced (or slow) vital capacity < 50% of predicted values (sitting or supine) as signs needing ventilation assistance. Note the 50% threshold differs from the 25% supine drop above.
- **Symptoms of hypoventilation:** A review says Hypercapnia causes compromising clinical symptoms such as sleep disturbances, daytime fatigue, cognitive impairment, and depression. Orthopnea and dyspnea are described as appearing In later stages.
- **Symptom–measurement link:** A REVEALS study found that the probability of reporting symptoms including dyspnea, orthopnea, and difficulty clearing secretions increased with decreasing respiratory measurement scores.
- **Caution:** One search result is a practice-question page citing NICE NG42 for the answer that NIV should be offered. It is not the guideline and I couldn't verify its claims, so check NG42 directly (nice.org.uk/guidance/ng42) for the official respiratory thresholds and any supine or morning-symptom criteria.
