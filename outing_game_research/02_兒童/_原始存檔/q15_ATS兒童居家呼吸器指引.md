## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The 2016 ATS guideline covers home care, caregiver training, and equipment, but these results don't address school or travel recommendations.

**Caregivers:** The guideline's recommendations include a trained caregiver being present in the home at all times, with the caveat that most families need a professional in-home caregiver so family members can sleep, work, and keep a life balance. It also suggests that at least two specifically trained family caregivers are prepared to care for the child in the home. The workgroup stressed that a lone trained family caregiver would rarely be capable of shouldering the entire burden of care.

**Discharge and home management:** The guideline suggests standardized discharge criteria to objectively assess readiness for care in the home. It also recommends a comanaged medical home, with a generalist and respiratory subspecialist working together.

**Equipment and monitoring:** The guideline calls for regular maintenance of home ventilators and all associated equipment as outlined by the manufacturer. For monitoring, it suggests a pulse oximeter rather than use of a cardiorespiratory monitor or sole use of the ventilator alarms, especially when the child is asleep or unobserved.

**Sources:** The full guideline is in the PMC article (PMC5439679), and Guideline Central lists the publication as April 1, 2016. The ATS also produced a family-facing handout, which mentions that family-centered care considers school, community life, and other family issues, but it doesn't give specific school or travel recommendations. A follow-up search of the full guideline text or its educational-objectives table would be needed to find those.
