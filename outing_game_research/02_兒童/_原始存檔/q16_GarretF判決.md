## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The Supreme Court decided this case on March 3, 1999, and its holding went against the school district. The Court held that the IDEA's related-services provision required the district to pay for nursing services that a ventilator-dependent student needed during the school day. The Court affirmed the Court of Appeals' ruling that these services are covered as related services, and the Eighth Circuit's decision is reported at 106 F.3d 822.

The student, Garret F., was paralyzed from the neck down after an accident and depended on a ventilator. He needed certain physical help to attend school and continuous one-to-one nursing care during the school day.

The Court leaned heavily on its earlier decision in Irving Independent School District v. Tatro. The judges relied heavily on Irving Independent School District v. Tatro. One reason the Court gave for treating the services as related services was that they did not require a physician's expertise. This distinction was crucial in determining that the services required by Garret F., such as ventilator support and catheterization, did not necessitate a physician's expertise and thus fell within the scope of related services. Disability advocates considered the Court decision to be a "substantial victory for families of children with disabilities."
