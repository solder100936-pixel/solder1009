## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search turned up useful material, but almost all of it concerns children, not adults, and I found no adult-specific training-hour requirements or ambu-bag return-demonstration checklist.

**Pediatric standards that may transfer:**
- A proposed discharge standard for invasively ventilated children requires caregivers to show competence in caring for and replacing the tracheostomy, and to recognize and respond to tube obstruction, decannulation, and bleeding. It also calls for at least two family caregivers to be fully trained. (Caregivers must demonstrate competency in the care and replacement of their child's tracheostomy... At least two family caregivers must be fully trained in all aspects of the child's care.)
- A Canadian hospital discharge checklist includes a self-inflating resuscitation bag at the bedside and caregiver education completed using the family's own home equipment. (Self inflating resuscitation bag to be with patients at all times)
- A Delphi-derived competency checklist, the KidsVent checklist, was built for pediatric caregivers. (In the final checklist, the 19 domains comprised 14 mandatory (189 mandatory items) and 5 optional domains (57 optional items).) I did not see its item-level content in the results.

**Return demonstration evidence:** A study on tracheostomy tube changes reported that caregivers who received demonstration and then performed a return demonstration improved substantially. Their mean score rose from near zero to about 77% after 72 hours. (After 72 h of demonstration, the score was 12.34 ± 1.6 (77.2%).) That study used a 16-step procedure. (The whole procedure was divided into 16 steps.) It is a small study and not an adult-specific source.

**Adult gap:** The results did not give adult home-ventilation hour requirements or an adult competency checklist. For adult guidance, search for home ventilation program discharge criteria from adult pulmonary or respiratory societies, or specific home-ventilation program manuals.
