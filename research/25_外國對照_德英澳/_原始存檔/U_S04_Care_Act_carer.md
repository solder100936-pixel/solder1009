## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search turned up the Act's core structure and several council policies on respite charges. The statute and official guidance are the authoritative sources.

**Assessments and eligibility**
- A carer can request a carer's assessment, and the Act gives them a free detailed assessment of your needs as a carer.
- Under section 10, a carer's assessment is triggered where the authority thinks a carer may have needs for support, and it applies regardless of the carer's financial resources (where it appears to a local authority that a carer may have needs for).
- Section 13 requires the authority, once an assessment is done, to decide whether needs meet the eligibility criteria (it must determine whether any of the needs meet the eligibility criteria). Regulation 3 of the Care and Support (Eligibility Criteria) Regulations 2014 sets the carer criteria, including needs that arise from providing necessary care for an adult (Regulation 3 of the Care and Support (Eligibility Criteria) Regulations 2014 sets out the eligibility criteria for carers).
- Section 20 duties to meet a carer's needs apply where the cared-for adult is ordinarily resident in the authority's area (The adult needing care is ordinarily resident in the local authority's area).

**Respite and the cared-for person's assessment**
- Respite is often tied to the cared-for person's own needs assessment. One council policy says the need for Respite Care is identified through the cared for person's Care Act Assessment.
- Another notes that a carer's assessment is currently the only way of providing statutory access to respite. Treat that as a commentary claim from a charity, not a statutory rule.

**Charges**
- Councils differ. One London borough states No Financial Assessment is required for Respite Care as carers are not asked for a financial contribution from Adult Social Care. Check your own council's charging policy, because the cared-for person may still be charged.
- Section 14 gives authorities the power to charge, and the Act's charging framework is set out in sections 14 to 17 (Charging and assessing financial resources 14 Power of local authority to charge 15 Cap on care costs).

**Caveats**
- Most results were council webpages, policies, or charity FAQs rather than the statute or official guidance. Verify specifics against the Act (legislation.gov.uk) and the Care and Support Statutory Guidance, and against your council's current charging policy.
