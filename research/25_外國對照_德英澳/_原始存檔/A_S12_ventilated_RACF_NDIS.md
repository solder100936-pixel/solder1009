## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search didn't turn up anything that directly addresses placing ventilator-dependent tracheostomy patients in Australian residential aged care. It does cover the general framework.

**Age and aged care rules:** The Department of Health says younger people may only live in a residential aged care home if other support options are not suitable or available for their care needs, and they must first explore other, age-appropriate accommodation and supports. Under the new Aged Care Act, if you are under 65 you can only access Australian Government-funded aged care services if you have care needs and meet further criteria, including a permanent disability that affects daily life activities.

**NDIS pathway:** The NDIS says if you're an NDIS participant under 65, you don't have to stay living in residential aged care. Supports for a participant who entered residential aged care before 65 may not be included. The Ability First Australia Younger People in Residential Aged Care System Coordinator Program can help people explore their options if they're under 65, not yet NDIS participants, and either living in or considering residential aged care.

**Ventilation-specific supports:** Victoria's Yooralla describes a NDIS-funded Ventilator Accommodation Support Service that provides accommodation and 24-hour support for people chronically dependent on mechanical ventilation. In NSW, the Home Ventilation Program provides funding assistance for adults and children dependent on mechanical ventilation who need significant care to leave hospital and be cared for at home. Under the NDIS, a participant must have a ventilator management support plan in place to receive ventilator support.

**Gaps:** Nothing here addresses how aged care homes actually handle ventilated residents, staffing requirements, or the barriers that cause placement failures. Searching for the Ability First YPIRAC program, state home ventilation programs, or NDIS ventilator-accommodation options would likely give more targeted answers.
