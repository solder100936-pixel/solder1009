The search covered the End-of-Life Pathway well but returned nothing on Carer Gateway respite or on how Australian aged care is funded through Commonwealth taxation, so those parts of your query are unanswered.

**End-of-Life Pathway basics:** The Government's own fact sheet says the pathway gives about $25,000 for home care services over a 12-week period to an older person accessing the End-of-Life Pathway. It can be extended to 16 weeks if funds remain, and you can access this for a total of 16 weeks, if those funds are still available. It targets people with a life expectancy of three months or less supporting dignity, comfort and care at home with their families.

**Key rules:**
- Use is limited to once. One source says an older person can access the End-of-Life Pathway once through the Support at Home program. A commercial provider's page instead claims no lifetime cap on access, which conflicts with the official position.
- Existing participants move onto it through a review. Existing Support at Home participants can request an urgent Support Plan Review to transition to the pathway. New participants usually need an aged care assessment.
- Care beyond the pathway falls back to ongoing services. If you need care beyond 3 months, you can access Support at Home ongoing services.
- A planned extension: the Department says from 8 March 2027, the End-of-Life Pathway will be extended to provide continued support for participants who remain living at home after the initial 12 weeks. I didn't find further detail on this, so check the Department's page before relying on it.

For Carer Gateway respite and the funding question, a follow-up search would be needed.
