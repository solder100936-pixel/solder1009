## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search returned no 2026-specific changes to the process, so the points below reflect the current framework as described in these sources. Most of the material is from local council and ICB pages, so check gov.uk for the latest version.

- **Two stages:** A screening checklist comes first, and a full Decision Support Tool assessment follows only if the checklist suggests possible eligibility. The full assessment process for NHS continuing healthcare usually involves 2 steps, the checklist and then the decision support tool.
- **Low bar at screening:** The checklist threshold has intentionally been set low. This is to ensure that all those who require a full assessment of eligibility for NHS continuing healthcare have this opportunity. A positive result doesn't confirm funding; it only opens the door to a full assessment.
- **Primary health need:** Eligibility is tied to whether someone has a 'primary health need', assessed with the checklist and decision support tool from the National Framework.
- **Needs, not diagnosis:** Your eligibility for NHS continuing healthcare depends on your assessed needs, and not on any particular diagnosis or condition.
- **Decision support tool:** The decision support tool sets out 12 areas to examine, that will be graded in accordance with your assessed level of need. The nature, complexity, intensity and unpredictability of your overall needs are also considered.
- **Timing:** ICBs will normally make a decision about eligibility for NHS continuing healthcare within 28 days of getting a completed checklist or request for a full assessment, unless circumstances beyond their control apply.
- **Care setting:** Funding is described as social care arranged and funded solely by the NHS. None of the sources I found say explicitly whether the funding covers a care home or a person's own home. That point needs checking against the national framework on gov.uk, since the setting generally depends on the individual's assessed needs and care plan.
- **Fast track:** A separate fast-track pathway exists for people at the end of life, and the sources point to a gov.uk fast track tool for this.
