## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search results point to a Single Assessment System (SAS) that replaced ACAT, with wait times that are still long but reportedly shortening. The sources disagree on some dates and figures.

- **Start date:** Several sources say the SAS workforce replaced ACAT and RAS assessors on 9 December 2024. The government replaced the previous ACAT and RAS systems with a single, unified process called the Single Assessment System. One ANAO page instead says the single assessment system commenced in December 2025, which looks like an error.
- **Current queue:** Data for the Single Assessment System shows there were 98,606 people waiting for an aged care assessment as of 31 March 2026. A later report says the backlog was 82,656 waiting at June.
- **Assessment wait:** One guide says a comprehensive assessment is usually arranged within 2 to 6 weeks after the 1 November 2025 reform. Media reports describe much longer waits, with some people waiting up to nine months to be assessed – four times the 40-day target.
- **Home care package wait:** The official Aged Care Act 2024 Wait Times Report measures application to service start. For the period to 31 March 2026, it found older Australians are waiting on average 12 months for a Support at Home package. The My Aged Care portal estimate for Standard priority packages has fallen to seven to eight months. The government has set a goal of average wait times for Support at Home of three months by 1 July 2027.
- **Latest official report:** The Quarter 4 (April to June 2026) report was published: 11 August 2026. It notes that the analysis is based on retrospective data, the findings may not reflect current system pressures or wait times.

Caveats: several results are secondary sites (guides and news outlets) with inconsistent publication dates, so the official Department of Health, Disability and Ageing and My Aged Care figures should be checked before relying on any single number.
