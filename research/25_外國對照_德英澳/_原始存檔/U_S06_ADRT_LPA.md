## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search confirms the core rule for England and Wales: an advance decision refusing life-sustaining treatment has formal requirements that ordinary advance decisions don't.

- **Formalities:** The decision and the statement confirming it must be in writing, signed, and the signature witnessed. Both the decision and the statement verifying it must be in writing and be signed and the signature must be witnessed. Some guidance adds that the witness should also sign in the person's presence. One source says the witness signs in front of you, while another says the person must sign in the presence of a witness, and the witness must date the document too.
- **Explicit wording:** The decision must state that it applies even if life is at risk. One source puts it this way: the advance decision is to apply to the specified treatment, even if life is at risk. Sources differ slightly on whether this statement must be in the main document or can be a separate signed and witnessed statement. The Code of Practice is the authority to check on that point.
- **Other advance decisions:** Refusals of other treatment can be oral and need not be witnessed. All other advance decisions can be made verbally and need not be witnessed if they are in writing.
- **Interaction with an LPA for health and welfare:** A later LPA can override an earlier advance decision. If a Lasting Power of Attorney for health and welfare is later made and registered which gives an attorney the power to give or refuse consent to any treatment covered by the advance decision, this will supersede the advance decision. The reverse also applies. In one scenario, an advance decision made after the LPA overrode the attorney's contrary request, and the attorney's best-interests authority could not justify the treatment. That source is a teaching case, not formal law.
- **Mental Health Act:** Where a person is detained under the Mental Health Act 1983, refusals of treatment for a mental disorder can be overridden, but this does not apply to physical health treatment. When the advance decision concerns refusal of treatment for a physical health problem, the Mental Health Act is not relevant and the Mental Capacity Act applies.

The sources are mainly UK guidance pages and practitioner articles. For a definitive answer, check the Mental Capacity Act 2005 Code of Practice, Chapter 9, and the statute itself (ss. 24–26) on legislation.gov.uk. The search did not cover Scotland or Northern Ireland, which have different rules.
