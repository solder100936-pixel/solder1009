## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search turned up a consistent picture of the expected-death pathway in England, though most sources are NHS trust leaflets or hospice guides rather than official government guidance.

- **Verification:** A GP or registered nurse usually performs the checks for a death at home. If the person died at home, these checks will usually be done by a GP or registered nurse. Nurses can only verify an expected death, and only where a local policy allows it. A registered nurse (RN) can verify that an expected death has occurred if there is a local policy to support the registered nurse.
- **Paperwork:** The verifying clinician completes a form confirming the death, which lets the funeral director collect the body. One leaflet describes the verification of expected death form (VOED) as the document that is signed by the health professional who confirms the death.
- **Medical certificate and examiner review:** A doctor issues the medical certificate of cause of death (MCCD). In England and Wales it is then reviewed by a medical examiner, who needs to be satisfied about the cause of death before a doctor can issue the Medical Certificate of Cause of Death (MCCD). Examiners are senior doctors, usually with experience in pathology, general practice or hospital medicine.
- **Registration timing:** Sources disagree on the clock. One says the death must be registered within 5 days (excluding weekends and bank holidays) of the MCCD reaching the register office. Another, from SAIF, says registration must happen within five days of the Medical Examiner signing the Medical Certificate Cause of Death (MCCD) which includes weekends and bank holidays. I could not resolve this from the results, so check gov.uk before relying on either.
- **Coroner referral:** Expected deaths can still go to the coroner, which can delay the certificate. Some examples are industrial related disease, which goes directly to the coroner rather than through the medical examiner process. A practice guide notes that medical examiner reviews and potential coroner referrals may delay the issuance of the MCCD, which in turn affects registration and funeral arrangements.

Caveat: none of the results were official gov.uk guidance on the 5-day rule or current referral criteria, and some items are older (a 2021 hospice slide deck, COVID-era guidance). Verify the registration deadline and coroner criteria against gov.uk or the Medical Examiner Service before relying on them.
