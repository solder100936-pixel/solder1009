## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search turned up useful detail on the 12-week property disregard, but nothing on the personal expenses allowance or on how a spouse living at home affects the assessment.

- **Basic rule:** Councils usually count the value of your home when you move into a care home, but may ignore it for 12 weeks. When you move into a care home to live there, we usually include the value of your home. We may ignore the value of your home for 12 weeks.
- **Capital threshold (East Sussex, April 2026):** After 12 weeks, the home's equity counts. If that pushes total capital above £23,250, you pay the full cost of care. After 12 weeks, we will include the equity in your home. If this means you now have more than £23,250, you will pay the full cost of your care. Other councils use different thresholds. Renfrewshire's is £35,500, and that is a Scottish council, so the figure differs by area.
- **Eligibility:** In East Sussex, you can get this if you are moving into a care home to live there and you have not already been paying the care home fees privately, and you moved directly from your home into the care home. Kent says the disregard covers only the value of the property. The person will still need to pay towards the cost of their residential/nursing care based on their other capital and income.
- **Spouse or dependants:** One source says if the person entering care owns their home and it is empty (no spouse or dependent living there), the property is disregarded from the financial assessment for the first 12 weeks. This suggests a spouse still living at home changes the position, but the results don't say how. That would more likely be a separate, longer-term disregard, so check with the local council.
- **Repeat disregards:** Leicestershire says you can only have one 12-week property disregard in any 52-week period.
- **Personal expenses allowance:** The results contain no 2026/27 figure. Bath & North East Somerset notes that in addition to your personal expense allowance during the 12 week property disregard period you will be allowed the costs of certain housing costs such as rent or mortgage. Check the current rate on GOV.UK or your council's site.
