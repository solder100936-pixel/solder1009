## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
Based on the search, an expected home death in Australia is not an emergency, and you don't need to call 000 or the police. Call the person's GP or palliative care team, who can arrange for a doctor to attend and issue the certificate. They can arrange for a doctor to visit to confirm the person's death and issue a medical certificate of cause of death. There is no legal requirement for you to contact anyone straight away. You do not need to contact the ambulance or police for an expected home death.

Key steps:

- **Medical certificate:** The certificate is needed before the funeral director can take the body. A Medical Certificate Cause of Death is the document needed before any funeral director can take the deceased person into their care. In Victoria, the Medical Certificate Cause of Death must be completed within 48 hours of death.
- **NSW nurse option:** In NSW, if a doctor isn't available, an authorised nurse or paramedic can complete a 'Verification of Death' form which confirms that the person has died, and allows the doctor to complete the Medical Certificate of Cause of Death in the next 48 hours.
- **Coroner:** If the doctor cannot confirm the cause of death, they will have to call the police. The police refer 'reportable deaths' to the coroner. A coroner referral is uncommon for an expected death at home.
- **Registration:** The doctor's certificate, noting the cause of death, goes to the state or territory Registry of Births, Deaths and Marriages. The registry then issues a death certificate. Note that the Medical Certificate is not the same as the death certificate. The Death Certificate is issued after the death is registered with the appropriate registration authority.
