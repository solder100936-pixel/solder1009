## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search turned up the main rules, but not the 2019 Regulations text itself. The official gov.uk guidance for doctors is the most authoritative source here.

- **Deadline:** The gov.uk guidance says deaths must be registered within 5 days of occurring, unless there is a coroner's post-mortem or inquest (index 3-1). Some councils instead count the 5 days from when the register office receives the medical certificate (index 10-1, 11-2), and Bath and North East Somerset counts 5 calendar days from when the register office receives the doctor's paperwork (index 17-1). Councils also note that the 5 days include weekends and bank holidays (index 5-2).
- **When the coroner must be told:** The coroner must be informed if the certifying doctor has not seen the deceased in the 28 days before death, and has not seen them after death either (index 7-2). Video consultations count toward the 28 days, but a telephone call does not (index 3-3). If the cause of death is unknown, the death must also be referred to the coroner (index 3-4).
- **Next steps after a referral:** The coroner decides whether any further investigation is needed before registration can happen (index 3-5). The coroner may also accept the doctor's certificate without further steps (index 3-6).
- **Medical examiner:** Some sources say the cause of death must be agreed with the Medical Examiner before registration (index 12-2). One source describes a 2026 process where the 5 days run from the medical examiner releasing the paperwork (index 8-2). That source is a secondary wiki page, and its citation is unverified, so I would not rely on it.

Overall, the 5-day clock and the 28-day rule are consistent across sources, but the exact starting point for the 5 days varies between official guidance and local council pages. Checking the gov.uk "register a death" page directly would confirm the current position.
