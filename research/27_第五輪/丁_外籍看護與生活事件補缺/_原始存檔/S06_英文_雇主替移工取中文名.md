## 回傳文字（原樣）
The search didn't turn up a study specifically on Taiwanese employers giving migrant caregivers Chinese names. The results do cover related research on how these workers are treated and perceived.

- **Identity and status:** Isabelle Cheng's paper "We want productive workers, not fertile women" examines how the state shapes the identities of migrant women caregivers, and how they are cast as outsiders in Taiwan's guest worker system. It may be the closest match to your topic, since identity is central to its argument, but the results don't mention names.
- **Employer-caregiver relationships:** Munkejord, Ness and Silan's 2021 article, "We Are All Interdependent," is a peer-reviewed study of live-in carers and their employers in Taiwan. The excerpts don't address naming.
- **Labor conditions:** Chen-Fen Chen's conference presentation, "Management or Exploitation?", reports interviews with employers of family migrant caregivers and finds that the management of these workers has become exploitative. Its findings include pay below the minimum wage and few regular rest days.
- **Naming practices:** A University of Edinburgh thesis looks at anglicized and Chinese names among overseas Taiwanese, but it concerns Taiwanese people abroad, not migrant workers in Taiwan.

A more targeted search, such as the Chinese-language terms for "foreign caregiver" with "naming" or "Chinese name," or a search of the Cheng paper's full text, would be the next step.
