- **Thị is the key middle name.** Several sources call it the most common female middle name, with Văn the male equivalent. One says it is much more common among the pre-1975 generation than among younger Vietnamese, and that people now often drop it. Thị is a most common female middle name, and most common amongst pre-1975 generation but less common amongst younger generations. Another notes that people named Thị sometimes omit their middle name.
- **Given names are usually what people go by.** Vietnamese people are nearly always referred to by first or given names, more rarely by full names, and almost never by family name alone. The Nguyễn Thị Vy example makes the same point: people will address this person as Vy and she also introduces herself as Vy.
- **Lan, Linh and Hoa appear in common-name lists, but not as female-specific rankings.** One list includes Anh, An, Châu, Dương, Giang, Hà, Hải, Khánh, Lan, Liem, Linh, Nhân, Minh, Ninh, Thanh, Tường, Quý, and Xuan as common names for both genders. Another lists Anh, Dung, Hạnh, Hoà, Hồng, Khánh, Lân, Liêm, Nhung, Duy and Xuân as used by both genders. Because these lists aren't split by sex, they don't establish which names are most common for women.
- **Many given names are unisex.** While certain types of names are more likely to be given to male or female children, most Vietnamese given names are effectively unisex and can be used by men or women. The Thị middle name is what usually signals gender.
- **Two-syllable given names are more common for women.** Some personal names may be two syllables/words long. For example, NGUYỄN Thị Hoa Điệp's personal name is 'Hoa Điệp'. This is more common for female names.
