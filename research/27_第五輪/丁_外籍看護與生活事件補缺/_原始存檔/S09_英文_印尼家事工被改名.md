## 回傳文字（原樣）
The search didn't find any source that addresses employers renaming Indonesian domestic workers or calling them by nicknames, so I can't confirm that practice or its scale from these results. The results do cover the broader setting:

- **Scale:** As of 2018, approximately 190,000 Indonesian migrant workers were employed as caregivers or domestic helpers in Taiwan, making up 76 per cent of all domestic workers in Taiwan.
- **Hong Kong:** Indonesian women make up almost half of the 300,000 migrant domestic workers employed in Hong Kong. Amnesty International's 2013 report examines a series of human and labour rights violations in the recruitment and employment of these workers.
- **Employer conduct:** One source, a 2019 piece by a UC Berkeley sociology researcher who studies the domestic labour system in both Taiwan and Hong Kong, notes that employers will often scold their domestic worker, raising their voices and yelling when she doesn't do things correctly. It doesn't mention renaming.
- **Taiwan report:** A study titled "Triple Whammy" covers some major exploitative practices on Indonesian migrant workers in Taiwan, but the retrieved text doesn't show whether it discusses naming practices.

For a more targeted search, try the "Triple Whammy" report directly, or search for terms like "maid naming" or "Indonesian domestic workers forced new names" alongside Taiwan or Hong Kong. Academic work on the "maid trade" in those places may also discuss naming and identity.
