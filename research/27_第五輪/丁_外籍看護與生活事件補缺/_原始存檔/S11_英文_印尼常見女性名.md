The search turned up a few rankings, but most sources are low-quality blogs, so treat the exact ranks with caution.

- **Ranking data:** A Japanese-language document (Okayama prefecture) lists SITI as the top female name, with DEWI fourth, PUTRI seventh and AYU eighth. It places NUR third for women and seventh for men. It is unclear which underlying dataset this uses.
- **Other lists:** A Tempo article on the 20 most popular names for males and females in Indonesia is circulated in other sources, though its female list in the results (Nurhayati, Sulastri, Sumiati, Sri Wahyuni and others) is unranked.
- **Nur:** Its meaning is "light" in Arabic, and it is used for both women and men.
- **Siti and Sri as single names:** Siti is described as a name that embodies respect and honor, often used to denote a woman of noble character or status. Its common use alongside other names, as in Siti Aminah, suggests it often functions as a title rather than a standalone mononym. Sri is similar: Sanskrit, Javanese meaning 'radiance', 'prosperity', or a title for the goddess Lakshmi.
- **Dewi:** It is associated with the goddess Dewi Sri, regarded as the goddess of rice and fertility.

Overall, Siti, Dewi and Nur are consistently among the most common female names. Siti and Sri are frequently used as honorifics before a second name, so whether they count as true single-name mononyms depends on the person. No source gave a reliable count of how many women use each name as their only name.
