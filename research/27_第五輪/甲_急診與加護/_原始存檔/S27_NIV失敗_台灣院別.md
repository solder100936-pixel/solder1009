## 回傳文字（原樣）
I didn't find a Chang Gung or Taichung study that reports a noninvasive ventilation (NIV) failure or intubation percentage for hypercapnic COPD. The results show Taiwanese retrospective studies, but none of the snippets give that figure.

- **Taipei Tzu Chi Hospital (2022):** A retrospective study of acute hypoxemic and hypercapnic respiratory failure defined NPPV failure as needing invasive ventilation after an NPPV trial. Liang YR, Lan CC, Su WL, Yang MC, Chen SY, Wu YK are the authors, and the snippets I saw don't include a failure rate. Two of the authors list Chang Gung affiliations in the journal record, but the snippets don't show a Chang Gung-based cohort. The decision of NPPV application is critical as ARF patients with failed NPPV have various worse outcomes than patients receiving primary IMV.
- **National Taiwan University Hospital Hsin-Chu Branch (2021):** A single-center retrospective cohort of AECOPD patients on NIV, focused on weaning predictors rather than intubation rates. Weaning failure was defined to include repeated NIV, intubation, high-flow oxygen, or long-term NIV support. Decisions regarding the duration of NIV and whether to progress to endotracheal intubation were made by the clinical team, based on the attending physician's judgement.
- **Non-Taiwan comparators:** An Italian study reported NIV failure, defined as needing intubation, of 19% in COPD versus 47% in other causes of hypercapnic failure (The risk of NIV failure, defined as the need for endotracheal intubation, was significantly lower in COPD than in other conditions (19% vs. 47%).). A Korean ED review reported that failure rates of 5–50 % have been reported across studies.

For the Taiwan-specific number, check the full texts of the Tzu Chi and NTUH studies or search PubMed for Chang Gung or Taichung COPD NIV cohorts directly.
