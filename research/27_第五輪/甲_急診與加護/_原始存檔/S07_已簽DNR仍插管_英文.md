## 回傳文字（原樣）
The search didn't turn up a study that directly measures CPR or intubation given to ED patients who had already signed a Taiwanese DNR. It did find related Taiwanese work on DNR orders in the emergency department.

- **Legal framework:** Under the Hospice Palliative Care Act, doctors can only forgo life-sustaining treatment, such as intubation, CPR, or resuscitation drugs, when terminal patients or their families sign formal written consents. The right to refuse CPR/IMV is only entitled to terminal patients, which matters if a patient signs a DNR but isn't clearly terminal.
- **Early DNR and intubation:** A Taiwan Stroke Registry analysis notes that if a DNR consent is signed before intubation, the patient would not be intubated, and this DNR order would be classified as a DNR without IMV (early DNR).
- **ED DNR and outcomes:** A Taipei Veterans General Hospital study found that EDNR patients underwent lesser endotracheal tube (ET) intubation (15.6% vs. the comparison group, with the snippet cut off). They also had reduced epinephrine injection, ventilator support, and narcotic use during end-of-life care, as well as decreased length of hospital stay, hospital expenditure, and survival compared to late DNR patients. The search snippet doesn't show the full comparison, so check the paper before relying on these numbers.
- **Kaohsiung study:** A study of ED DNR orders and respiratory failure after ICU admission reported that signing a DNR order was associated with high medical fees among survivors. The snippets don't show whether intubation occurred despite a DNR.
- **Waiting times:** A northern Taiwan study found that patients who signed a DNR earlier experienced longer waiting times in the emergency department.

The search didn't surface any study that quantifies CPR or intubation performed despite a signed DNR in Taiwan's EDs. The closest leads are the Taipei Veterans and Kaohsiung papers, which are probably the best starting points for a full-text check.
