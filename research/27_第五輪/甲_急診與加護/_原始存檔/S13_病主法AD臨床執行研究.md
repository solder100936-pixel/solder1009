## 回傳文字（原樣）
The search didn't turn up studies on the Act's use in terminal irreversible coma cases. What it did find is mostly about advance directive (AD) uptake in Taiwan.

- **Pre-Act baseline:** A 2022 nationwide retrospective review of 3,004 death records from 18 hospitals (BMC Palliative Care) found that 79% had signed ADs, with most (95%) being signed by patients' caregivers. The authors studied this before enactment of Patient Right to Autonomy Act, so it describes the period before the Act's effects.
- **Post-Act single-center study:** A 2021 retrospective analysis from Taipei Veterans General Hospital examined motivations for advance care planning. It reports that up to ten thousand declarants have participated in advance care planning (ACP) and have signed advance directives (ADs) since the Act took effect in 2019. It covered one year of data at one center, so it is not a national picture.
- **Eligibility:** The same study notes that only individuals who are 20 years of age and over and with full disposing capacity may sign an AD.
- **Gaps:** Results describe a barrier to ACP conversations, with one source noting that even with the passage of the Act, it remained difficult to initiate a discussion on ACP. Nothing retrieved addresses how the Act's advance decisions were applied in terminal irreversible coma, or includes case-level clinical implementation data. Searching for the Act's text, its 2019 implementing rules, or Taiwanese studies on withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment in coma would be the next step.
