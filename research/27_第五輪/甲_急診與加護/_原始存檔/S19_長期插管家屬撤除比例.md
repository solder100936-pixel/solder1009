## 回傳文字（原樣）
The search turned up several Taiwanese studies on this topic, though none that directly reports the proportion of respiratory care center patients whose families chose withdrawal or hospice.

- **Crit Care Med 2017 (Taipei City Hospital system):** This cross-sectional study enrolled family members of 106 patients on prolonged ventilation across five hospitals. Most families accepted the concept of palliative care (90.4%). About 17.3% agreed to ventilator withdrawal currently, and 67.5% agreed to it in anticipation of death when terminal. Approximately half of the family members regretted having chosen prolonged mechanical ventilation (56.7%). Longer ventilation and hospitalization were linked to more regret. Sources: Determinants of Receiving Palliative Care and Ventilator Withdrawal Among Patients With Prolonged Mechanical Ventilation. Crit Care Med 2017; [PubMed](https://www.pubmed.gov/28658025/) and [Taiwan TMU record](https://hub.tmu.edu.tw/en/publications/determinants-of-receiving-palliative-care-and-ventilator-withdraw/).

- **Respiratory Care 2019 (integrated prospective payment program):** Among caregivers, 67.6% agreed to write a letter of intent about hospice or limited life-sustaining treatment, and 66.2% agreed to hospice care for a terminal family member. Only 70.6% knew about the Hospice Palliative Care Act. Source: [Open University CARE-N record](https://wels.open.ac.uk/research-project/caren/node/5623).

- **Qualitative study (Asian Pacific Journal of Ventilation/APM):** Interviews with 21 surrogate decision-makers of patients on prolonged ventilation described the stress of making these choices under time pressure. Source: [amegroups.org](https://apm.amegroups.org/article/view/46182/html).

- **Dalin Tzu Chi respiratory care center (tracheostomy decision program, 2017–2019):** This retrospective study covered 57 respiratory care center patients. Of participants, 91.2% felt they had made an optimal decision despite the outcome. Source: [PMC7735795](https://rcastoragev2.blob.core.windows.net/e623d7e9f00958e17bd5769a59c1445c/PMC7735795.pdf).

- **Respiratory care ward DNR study (American Journal of Hospice & Palliative Medicine, 2025):** This study examined family and nurse perceptions after DNR orders for ventilator-dependent patients in New Taipei City. Source: [EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/articles/health-and-medicine/808fbb07-3b0e-57bc-8fe0-a4823c2a8182/exploring-the-perceptions-of-families-and-nurses-after-signing-a-do-not-resuscitate-order-for-patients-in-respiratory-care-wards/).
