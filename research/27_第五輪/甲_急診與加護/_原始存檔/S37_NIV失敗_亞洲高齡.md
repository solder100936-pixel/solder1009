## 回傳文字（原樣）
The single search did not turn up a study that matches your exact criteria (age 80+, COPD exacerbation, Japan/Korea/China, retrospective, intubation proportion). No Japanese or Korean cohort appeared, and none reported a stratified intubation rate for patients 80 and older.

What the results did contain:

- **China (Beijing Hospital, 2022):** A retrospective study of 100 elderly patients with acute hypercapnic respiratory failure (not COPD-specific) found that concurrent severe pneumonia, chest X-ray infiltration in three or more quadrants, and a SAPS II score of 34 or higher were independent predictors of NIV failure. Elderly patients with AHRF face the risk of failure with NIV therapy. The abstract I saw does not give an overall failure rate.
- **Older-adult COPD cohorts:** One study defined NIV failure as intubation or death during hospitalization. It reported NIV success in 85,5% (group 1) and failure in 14,5% (group 2), and concluded that age is the only factor associated with NIV failure, with mean age of death of 84,6 yrs. This is a conference abstract with no stated geography, so I can't attribute it to Asia.
- **Cuban COPD cohort:** This series reported a failure rate of 65.3 %, with 85.7 % of the patients in whom non-invasive ventilation failed were over 60 years of age. The median age was 72, so it is not an elderly-specific estimate.
- **Narrative review (2025):** It notes that considering older patients (mean 80 years) with ARF, the in-hospital mortality was explicitly reported as 16%, and that data on very old patients and do-not-intubate patients remain limited and often conflicting.

Next steps: search PubMed or J-STAGE directly for Japanese multicenter NIV registries (e.g., JRC-type cohorts) and Korean COPD cohort studies (e.g., KOCOSS) with age-stratified outcomes. I'd also check whether Chinese COPD-specific retrospective series report intubation by age band.
