## 回傳文字（原樣）
The search turned up a 2021 retrospective study from Taipei Veterans General Hospital (TVGH) comparing early and late DNR orders in critically ill ED patients. I couldn't confirm the exact 15.6% comparison figure, because the snippet cuts off right after it.

- **Study design:** Researchers looked at 1,064 acutely ill patients admitted to TVGH's emergency intensive care unit between February 2018 and January 2020. 619 (58.2%) had EDNR and 445 (41.8%) LDNR. EDNR was defined as a DNR signed within 24 hours of ED presentation.
- **Intubation:** The snippet states that EDNR patients underwent lesser endotracheal tube (ET) intubation with the 15.6% figure beginning right after, but the comparison group's rate is missing from the excerpt.
- **Other end-of-life care:** The authors' conclusions say EDNR patients received less epinephrine, less ventilator support, and less narcotic use, along with shorter stays and lower survival than LDNR patients (decreased ET intubation and had reduced epinephrine injection, ventilator support, and narcotic use during EOL).
- **Survival:** EDNR patients had lower survival at 7, 30, and 90 days, with p-values of <0.001, <0.001, and 0.023 respectively.

A separate TVGH study of palliative-consultation-eligible ED patients with and without DNR orders reported more in-hospital deaths among the DNR group and more endotracheal extubations. Those results are in the same search, but they address a different question.

To verify the 15.6% figure and the LDNR comparison value, check the full text at the PMC or MDPI versions of the 2021 paper (PMC7908360 / MDPI, International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, vol. 18, issue 3).
