## 回傳文字（原樣）
I didn't find a Taiwan-specific figure for how many family members request nondisclosure. The closest Taiwanese data comes from a 2006 study and a smaller questionnaire study.

- **Largest Taiwan study (2006):** Researchers surveyed and interviewed 617 patient-family caregiver pairs across 21 hospitals. Their agreement on what had been disclosed was low, and the patients strongly asserted their own right to be informed. Six hundred seventeen dyads of patient-designated family caregivers across 21 hospitals throughout Taiwan were surveyed and interviewed by a semistructured interview guide. There were substantial discrepancies in the knowledge and experiences of being informed about the diagnosis and prognosis. Patients also preferred their physicians to inform themselves before releasing any information to their family caregivers.
- **Smaller Taiwan survey:** This Taipei study of 53 cancer inpatients and 66 family members found that the greatest factor influencing a family's willingness to tell the truth was the patient's emotional steadiness (89.1%). It also reported that the older the patient, the more unwillingness of his/her family to tell the truth.
- **Related Taiwan study (2004):** Researchers found that the common practice of nondisclosure of prognosis and detailed disease-related information by health-care professionals continues, even though patients expressed a strong preference for health-care professionals to inform them of disease-related information before disclosing information to their family members.
- **Outside Taiwan:** A Turkish study of relatives of 150 newly diagnosed cancer patients found that 66% did not want the diagnosis to be disclosed. Another, from Beijing, reported that family members often support nondisclosure, especially when they have experience with a relative who is unaware of the truth.

For an exact Taiwan percentage of families requesting nondisclosure, the full texts of the 2006 and 2004 Taiwan studies would be the next place to check.
