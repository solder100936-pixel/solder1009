## 回傳文字（原樣）
The search turned up useful Taiwan studies on weaning success and the integrated delivery system (IDS), but none reported reintubation rates, and I didn't find a single study with all the outcomes you listed.

- **Weaning rates:** A 2006–2008 retrospective study of a step-down respiratory care center found that 43.8% of patients on prolonged ventilation were weaned, and the oldest patients (80 and over) had lower weaning probability and poorer outcomes ([IJG, 2012](https://doaj.org/article/a7570ea1d03043b2b923d74e953ec6c1)). A hospital-based center study reported 56% of patients successfully weaned, with a shorter stay in the center linked to success ([Wu et al., Respir Med 2009](https://pure.lib.cgu.edu.tw/zh/publications/predictors-of-successful-weaning-from-prolonged-mechanical-ventil-3/)). A 2013–2018 central Taiwan center reported 56.0% successful weaning across 963 patients ([PMC8104124](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8104124)).
- **IDS/IPP policy effects:** A national claims-database analysis (2006–2010, about 30,000 matched patients) found the prospective payment program was linked to a higher survival rate but a lower weaning rate (HR 0.84), with higher costs ([PubMed 30097352](https://www.pubmed.gov/30097352/)). Another study noted the IDS began in 2000 and that patients who couldn't wean within 63 days were moved to respiratory care wards or home ventilation ([Airiti Library thesis](https://www.airitilibrary.com/Publication/Index/U0007-2407201415453400)).
- **Tracheostomy:** A long-term follow-up study concluded that tracheostomy should be attempted in suitable patients to improve long-term outcomes in successfully weaned patients ([Dove Press](https://www.dovepress.com/article/download/67332)). A separate study linked preadmission tracheostomy to better outcomes in the post-ICU respiratory care setting ([J Formos Med Assoc 2017](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/ref/10.2147/IJGM.S287529)), though I only saw that as a reference listing.
- **Reintubation:** No results addressed this. The Airiti thesis mentions high readmission rates among patients who had weaned, but that is readmission, not reintubation.

Coverage is also inconsistent across studies: weaning rates vary widely, and the sources have different denominators and follow-up windows. A targeted search for reintubation rates in Taiwan's respiratory care centers would be the next step.
