## 回傳文字（原樣）
I ran one search and did not find a Taiwanese study that reports a 72-hour reintubation rate specifically for elderly patients on prolonged mechanical ventilation. The closest matches are below.

- **Prolonged ventilation, HFNC vs. NIPPV (Tseng et al., *Scientific Reports*, 2023):** This single-center prospective study in Taipei compared high-flow nasal cannula and noninvasive ventilation for preventing reintubation in prolonged ventilation patients. Most of the patients were recruited from the medical ICU (85% in both groups). The HFNC group had more patients needing reintubation within 72 hours or 7 days (P = 0.047), while 90-day mortality did not differ significantly. The sample was small, with 90-day mortality of four patients [20%] vs. two patients [10%].
- **Failed extubation and age (Cheng et al., *International Journal of Gerontology*, 2010):** This Taiwanese ICU study defined failed extubation as reintubation within 48 hours rather than 72. Among 539 unplanned extubations, a failed UE rate of 48.2 (260/539) was reported, and older age was among the predictors of failure.
- **Planned extubation (Cheng et al., *International Journal of Gerontology*, 2011):** In a larger cohort of planned extubations, 167 patients (9.3%) required reintubation within 48 hours. Again, the window is 48 hours, not 72.
- **Extremely elderly (Medicine, 2020):** This retrospective Taiwanese cohort looked at patients over 90 who passed planned extubation after more than 24 hours of ventilation. I did not retrieve its reintubation figures.

The sources suggest that age and prolonged ventilation both raise the risk of extubation failure, but none of the results I retrieved give a 72-hour reintubation rate for elderly patients on prolonged ventilation in Taiwan. A targeted search of PubMed or the Taiwanese journals for "extubation failure" with "prolonged mechanical ventilation" and "elderly" would be the next step. Note also that the definitions of failure (48 hours, 72 hours, or 7 days) differ across these studies, so the rates are not directly comparable.
