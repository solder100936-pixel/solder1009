## 回傳文字（原樣）
I didn't find a study that matches your exact criteria (ventilation longer than 14 days, DNR, palliative consultation, and withdrawal before death). The search turned up several related Taiwanese medical-center studies, though.

- **DNR rates before death:** A 2016–2017 retrospective study of 386 ICU patients found that 319 (82.6%) signed a DNR before the end. A 2012 single-ICU chart review found a similar pattern, with DNR in 87% of patients who died, and the mean interval from DNR signature to death was 3.9 days.
- **Ventilator withdrawal with palliative consultation:** A retrospective observational study of ICU patients with terminal illness examined outcomes of palliative team consultation for ventilator withdrawal. It noted that ventilator withdrawal in the intensive care unit (ICU) is still a challenge. The search snippet doesn't include its numbers.
- **Timing of palliative consultation:** A medical-center study of patients who had endotracheal tube withdrawal compared an early-integration group (median survival of 22 days or more) with a late-referral group (under 22 days). Since the data were skewed to the right, the patients were split at the median survival time.
- **End-of-life resource use:** A teaching-hospital study of people who died in 2019–2021 counted ventilation, ICU stays, and CPR within 30 days before death. It found that initiating palliative care before a patient's last hospital admission was associated with less aggressive medical interventions in the 30 days before death.

The snippets don't give the specific proportion of patients ventilated for more than 14 days who had a palliative consultation or a withdrawal before death. The full texts of the Su et al. ventilator-withdrawal study and the 2022 IJERPH DNR study would be the next places to check.
