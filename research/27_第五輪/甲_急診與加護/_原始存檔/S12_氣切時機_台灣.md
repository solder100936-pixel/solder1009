## 回傳文字（原樣）
The search didn't turn up a nationwide Taiwan cohort that reports the proportion of patients tracheostomized by day of intubation. The closest matches are below.

- **Nationwide cohort (Lai et al., 2021):** This study used Taiwan's National Health Insurance Research Database to compare long-term mortality in prolonged ventilation patients with and without tracheostomy. It addresses survival and costs, not timing. This retrospective population-based study extracted data from the longitudinal National Health Insurance Research Database in Taiwan.
- **Single-center timing study (Tai et al., 2019):** This is not nationwide. It was run at a tertiary hospital in southern Taiwan. It compared early tracheostomy (≤14 days of intubation) with late (>14 days). The outcomes between different timing of tracheostomy (early: ≤14 days; late: >14 days of intubation) were compared. The authors also note that the tracheostomy timing for patients with prolonged mechanical ventilation (PMV) was usually delayed in our country.
- **Lin et al. (BMC Anesthesiology, 2015):** This study asked whether tracheostomy beats translaryngeal intubation for ICU patients ventilated for more than 14 days. The paper's background states that tracheostomy is recommended for patients receiving mechanical ventilation (MV) for 14 days or more in the intensive care unit (ICU).
- **Respiratory care center data:** A Taiwanese respiratory care center reported that the tracheostomy rate of PMV patients in the RCC medical center was about 39%. This is a single-center figure, not a national one.

For the specific nationwide proportion you want, the Taiwan NHIRD-based papers or the Lai et al. full text are the best places to check. Their methods or results sections may give a timing breakdown.
