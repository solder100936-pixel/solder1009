## 回傳文字（原樣）
I didn't find a multicenter Taiwan study that reports national percentages of ICU deaths involving withdrawal or withholding of life-sustaining treatment after the 2013 amendment. The results were single-center or single-hospital studies, so they can't give a nationwide figure.

- **Legal context:** One result notes that the Hospice Palliative Care Act was amended to let families withdraw life-sustaining treatment for unconscious terminally ill patients. It also says ventilator withdrawal in ICUs remains difficult in practice. The Hospice Palliative Care Act in Taiwan was amended and allowed families to withdraw life-sustaining treatment for unconsciousness terminally ill patients. However, ventilator withdrawal in the intensive care unit (ICU) is still a challenge.
- **Medical ICU, one center:** A thesis on terminally ill MICU patients found that the rates of consent for withdrawal of life support were 10.4% (34/326). That is a consent rate at one institution, not a share of deaths.
- **Pediatric ICU, one center:** A single-center study at a children's hospital (2011–2017) reported that 169 of 200 (85%) died after life-sustaining treatment was limited, and the other 31 of 200 (15%) died after withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment. This was among patients with a DNR order. Its abstract also reports that withdrawal rose from 4% to 31% (p < 0.001) over the study period.
- **Adult ICU, DNR and non-beneficial treatment:** A 2016–2017 retrospective study found that the life-sustaining treatments were withdrawn for only a few patients before death. Its abstract describes it as a single study without a national scope.

To find the multicenter figure you need, search for Taiwan Society of Critical Care Medicine or Taiwan Hospice Palliative Care Association registry data, or the national End-of-Life Care Act/Hospice Palliative Care Act evaluation reports from the Ministry of Health and Welfare.
