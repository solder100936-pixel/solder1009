## 回傳文字（原樣）
The search didn't turn up a study that reports the median days from intubation to tracheostomy in an NHIRD cohort, or the early tracheostomy proportion from that database. The closest results are below.

- **Timing in Taiwan generally:** One Taiwanese review reports that tracheostomy in critically ill patients there is often delayed, with mean times to tracheostomy ranging from 18.5 to 36.7 days. These are means, not medians, and the source doesn't say which data they came from.
- **Early vs. late definitions:** Several studies use a 14-day cutoff. One Taiwanese cohort defined early tracheostomy as within 14 days of intubation (≤14 days) and late as after 14 days, and reported that 57 subjects were in the early group and 77 in the late group. This is a single-center retrospective study, not NHIRD.
- **NHIRD-based data:** A 2013 analysis of NHI records for patients aged 45 and older found that the tracheostomy rate was 6.4% among patients who were mechanically ventilated. The snippet doesn't give timing relative to intubation.
- **Prolonged ventilation cohort:** A 2021 NHIRD study of prolonged mechanical ventilation found that of 10,705 patients included, 1372 underwent tracheostomy (n = 563) or translaryngeal intubation (n = 779). It doesn't report timing medians in the snippets.

For the exact median and the early-tracheostomy proportion, the next step is to look for full-text NHIRD studies on tracheostomy timing, especially those that report medians with interquartile ranges.
