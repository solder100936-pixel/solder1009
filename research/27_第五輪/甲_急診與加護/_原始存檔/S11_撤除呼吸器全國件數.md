## 回傳文字（原樣）
I found no national statistics on how many ventilated terminal patients have had life support withdrawn in Taiwan, either before or after the 2013 amendment. The results only cover the law and single-institution studies.

**What the amendment changed:** A 2013 Taipei Times report says the amendment lowered the threshold for withdrawing life-sustaining medical treatment for terminally ill patients. Under it, a terminal diagnosis confirmed by two specialist physicians and a letter of intent signed by the patient are the main requirements. Other sources date the change differently. One says the act amended on January 9, 2013, legalizes the procedure of CE in Taiwan, where CE means compassionate extubation. An earlier legal change is also reported: one source says the act was amended in 2002 to permit the withdrawal of life-sustaining devices from terminally ill patients, and another says discontinuing ventilation was not legally approved by the government until June 2002.

**Single-center data (not national):**
- A medical center study of one-hundred-thirty-five terminally ill patients who had mechanical ventilation withdrawn between 2013 and 2016 found that 102 patients (75.6%) died within one day after extubation.
- A community hospital reported 20 consultations for CE between September 2013 and September 2014, with eight cases excluded because of non-qualification.

The search did not return any national registry or Ministry of Health and Welfare count of withdrawals. A narrower search of Ministry or NHI statistical reports, or Taiwanese-language sources, would be the next step.
