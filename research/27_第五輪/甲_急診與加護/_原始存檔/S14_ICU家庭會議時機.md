## 回傳文字（原樣）
The search didn't turn up anything specific on timing, meaning when family meetings or palliative consultations happen relative to intubation, ICU admission, or the decision to withdraw. The results cover related Taiwanese ICU practice in general.

- **End-of-life ICU care in Taiwan:** In a chart-review study of 326 critically ill patients, most who died in the ICU kept life-sustaining treatment until death, and 88.3% died on mechanical ventilation. Palliative care consultations occurred in 73.3% of cases, and family was present at death in 76.1%. Patients predominantly (79.8%) continued life-sustaining treatments (LSTs) until death and died with full life support. The authors concluded that family meetings should be promoted to avoid unnecessary suffering from potentially inappropriate treatment in the final days. (Family meetings should be actively promoted to facilitate appropriate end-of-life-care decisions)

- **Prolonged mechanical ventilation:** A cross-sectional study found that 17.3% of family members agreed to ventilator withdrawal currently, while 67.5% agreed to it terminally, in anticipation of death (index 1-4). Greater family understanding of palliative care was associated with greater willingness to withdraw support (index 1-5).

- **Family conferences and caregiver distress:** A five-hospital Taipei study of 136 caregivers of ventilated patients found that more frequent palliative care family conferences were linked to more psychological symptoms among caregivers (index 10-3). Higher caregiver burden and lower baseline acceptance of palliative care predicted greater distress after death (index 10-4).

- **Policy context:** Palliative care, DNR orders, and formal family meetings were promoted by the Taiwanese government but not mandated, and bedside physician-family discussions were common (index 8-9, 8-10). The Hospice Palliative Care Act of 2000 governs DNR choices (index 4-9).

- **Telehealth conferences:** A pilot at a Taiwanese cancer center reported that telehealth palliative family conferences reached consensus on withdrawing life-sustaining treatment in 93% of cases (index 5-6).

For the timing question specifically, a targeted search on prolonged ventilation and palliative consultation timing in Taiwanese or Asian ICUs would be the next step.
