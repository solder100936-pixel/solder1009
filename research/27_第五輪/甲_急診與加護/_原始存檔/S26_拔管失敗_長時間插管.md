## 回傳文字（原樣）
I didn't find a Taiwanese retrospective study that uses a more-than-7-day intubation threshold, so the closest matches are several Chi Mei Medical Center studies that define failure as reintubation within 48 hours.

- **Planned extubation (2008 ICU data):** Among 1,794 planned extubations, 167 patients (9.3%) required reintubation within 48 hours. Older age and medical (non-surgical) patients predicted failure. The study was published in the International Journal of Gerontology in 2011.
- **Unplanned extubation (2004–2007):** This study found a failed UE rate of 48.2% (260/539). Longer intubation time was one of several predictors, alongside age, accidental extubation, and not being on a weaning trial (odds ratio 1.002 for intubation time).
- **Unplanned extubation (2009–2015):** 136 patients (46.1%) were re-intubated within 48 h.
- **Overweight/obese patients (2010–2011):** Extubation of 34 patients (5.7%) failed. The same study defined failure as reintubation within 48 hours of extubation.
- **2025 AI prediction study:** A single-center retrospective study of 5,202 adults who underwent elective extubation after SBT success aimed to predict extubation success within 48 hours using routine EMR data.

The failure rates vary widely, from about 5.7% to 48%, mainly because the studies differ in whether extubation was planned or unplanned. None of these results reports a rate specifically for patients intubated more than 7 days, so that figure would need a more targeted search or direct review of the full texts.
