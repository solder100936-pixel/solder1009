## 回傳文字（原樣）
I ran one search and didn't find a Taiwan study that matches all of your criteria (elderly patients, pneumonia, emergency department, retrospective). The closest Taiwan match is a multicenter retrospective study of COVID-19 patients, not a general pneumonia cohort.

- **Taiwan, COVID-19 (2021):** A multicenter retrospective study of adults with COVID-19 acute respiratory failure who needed invasive ventilation during Taiwan's first major outbreak. Of these, 107 received HFNC before intubation and 461 received conventional oxygen. Pre-intubation HFNC failure did not significantly affect hospital mortality but was associated with prolonged mechanical ventilation and ICU stays. The authors also concluded that HFNC use after extubation was linked to lower 28-day post-extubation mortality.
- **Elderly, ED-focused (Korea):** A single-university-hospital retrospective cohort of patients with hypoxic respiratory failure treated with HFNC at ED presentation found that older age (over 65 years), a Glasgow Coma Scale score below 15, and a respiratory rate above 30/min were significantly associated with HFNC failure on multivariable analysis. This is Korean, not Taiwanese, and the record is in Korean.
- **ED prediction tool:** A 2024 study in *Respiration* found that ROX and ROX-HR index values below the study cutoffs were associated with high risk of intubation after adjustment for confounders. The authors concluded these indices, measured before starting HFNC, offer a relatively fair predictive value for HFNC failure in the ED.
- **Elderly, general ARF (2024):** A review article on HFNC in elderly patients with acute respiratory failure, published in *Am J Transl Res*, notes that severe pneumonia is a common trigger for this condition in older adults. I did not confirm its country of origin or design from the excerpt.

A better follow-up search would be for Taiwanese ED or hospital cohorts of older adults with community-acquired pneumonia on HFNC, perhaps using Taiwan CDC or TMU/NTUH-affiliated terms.
