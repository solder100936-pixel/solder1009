## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search turned up the background and the commencement date, but not the final amended text, so check the Ordinance itself before relying on the exact conditions.

- **Before the change:** Hong Kong's Coroners Ordinance treated deaths in residential care homes as reportable to the coroner. One source says all cases of death at residential care homes are reportable to the coroner. Death at home from natural causes after a terminal diagnosis was generally not reportable, though a 2023 LegCo paper noted that a nursing home for which a licence is issued under the Residential Care Homes (Elderly Persons) Ordinance (Cap. 459) are not reportable deaths.
- **The amendment:** The change targets care homes. A government statement says the amended provisions are meant to facilitate terminally ill patients residing in RCHDs and RCHEs that are not nursing homes in opting for dying-in-place arrangements. A news report on the April 2024 gazettal describes a case where, if the resident's cause of death certificate states that he or she died of a natural cause, the case will no longer be considered as a reportable death to the Coroners Court.
- **Commencement:** The Coroners Ordinance (Amendment of Schedule 1) Notice 2023 took effect on 3 June 2024, per L.N. 42 of 2024.
- **Conditions vary by source:** A 2024 SCMP piece described the proposal as residential care homes do not have to report a death if the resident was seen by a doctor in the preceding 14 days, a final diagnosis was made, and the cause of death determined. That differs from the terminal-illness framing in the government and LegCo materials, so the final gazetted wording should be checked.

Sources: the LegCo panel paper (legco.gov.hk, 2023), the government press release (news.gov.hk, April 2024), the government LCQ17 reply (info.gov.hk, April 2026), and LegCo L.N. 42 of 2024.
