## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The Ordinance is already in force. The Government set July 31, 2026 as the commencement date, and it took effect on that day. An earlier 2025 government paper had said "January 2026," but the later official notices supersede that.

- **Commencement:** The Secretary for Health appointed July 31, 2026 in the Gazette on May 22, 2026. The Ordinance (Cap. 651) comes into effect today (July 31), providing a legal framework for advance medical directives (AMDs) and do-not-attempt cardiopulmonary resuscitation (DNACPR) orders.
- **Home and community relevance:** A DNACPR order, made by registered medical practitioners for a patient who has met the specified preconditions, seeks to ensure that, in settings outside hospitals and even when no healthcare professionals are present, rescuers will act in accordance with the relevant DNACPR order. This is the main provision that bears on dying at home.
- **Scope limits:** An AMD does not, however, allow the refusal of basic care or palliative care.
- **Existing documents:** Pre-existing AMDs made before the Ordinance commences will continue to be valid, as long as they comply with the requirements as stipulated in the Ordinance and the instructions in the AMD are clearly presented. DNACPR orders issued before the Ordinance commences must be replaced with the prescribed forms provided in the Ordinance in order to remain valid.
- **Electronic records:** The Government will progressively introduce electronic AMDs (eAMDs), with the Electronic Health System (eHealth) serving as the designated electronic system to support the making, storage, viewing and revocation of eAMDs.

I did not find anything specific about home-care or community-nursing programmes beyond these provisions, so that gap remains.
