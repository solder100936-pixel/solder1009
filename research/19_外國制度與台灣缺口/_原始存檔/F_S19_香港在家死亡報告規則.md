## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
Under Hong Kong's Coroners Ordinance (Cap. 504), a death at home is not automatically reportable. The key test is whether a doctor was involved, and the rules have a few layers:

- **Home deaths:** A death at home from natural causes generally need not be reported if the person was diagnosed with a terminal illness beforehand or a registered doctor saw them within 14 days before death. If a person without a terminal illness dies at home without being attended to by a registered medical practitioner within 14 days prior to death, the death then needs to be reported to the Coroner by law.
- **Schedule 1 category:** The Judiciary's summary lists medically unattended within 14 days prior to the death, except where the person was diagnosed as having a terminal illness as one of the reportable categories. A hospital's guidance says the same thing, and that all reportable deaths need to be reported to the police.
- **Who reports:** Under the Judiciary's table, the reporting duty generally falls on the doctor who certified the death or attended the last illness, or on any police officer. Anyone responsible for reporting a death to the Coroner should do so as soon as they reasonably can after they know of the death.
- **Separate registration duty:** Under the Births and Deaths Registration Ordinance, a death in a house must be reported to the registrar within 24 hours by the nearest relatives, persons present, or the occupier. This provides that where a person dies in a house, the nearest relatives, or each person present at the death, or every occupier of such house shall report the facts to the registrar within 24 hours. This is a 1990s-era report, so check it against the current text.
- **Recent changes:** The 2024 amendments to Schedule 1 concern residential care homes, not private homes. They took effect on June 3, 2024, and if a resident who passed away in an RCH was diagnosed as having a terminal illness while alive and was attended to by a registered medical practitioner within 14 days before passing away the death is no longer reportable, provided the cause certificate states a natural cause.

Caveat: Several sources are dated (some are 2023–2024 legislative papers), and I did not find a current consolidated text of Schedule 1 or a 2026 check. Verify the exact wording of paragraph 16 and the other Schedule 1 categories on the e-Legislation site before relying on this.
