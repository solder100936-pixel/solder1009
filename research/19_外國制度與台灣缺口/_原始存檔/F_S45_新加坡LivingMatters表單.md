Here's a brief summary of what the search turned up. It covers the Living Matters framework well, but I didn't find sources that use the exact terms "nominated healthcare spokesperson" or "surrogate" for Singapore's program.

- **Program basics:** The national programme launched in 2011 and is based on the Respecting Choices model from Gundersen Health System in Wisconsin. The Singapore government launched a comprehensive national ACP programme, 'Living Matters', in 2011. 'Living Matters' is based on the Respecting Choices programme at the Gundersen Health System in Wisconsin, USA.

- **Three ACP forms:** There were three different forms, general, disease specific, and the preferred plan of care form, to cater to individuals at different health stages (healthy, chronically ill, advanced illness). The PPC form is aimed at people with more advanced illness. One source says the Preferred-Plan-of-Care ACP is for a patient with more advanced illness(es). Given their conditions, such a patient may pass away within a year. Another calls it the Preferred Care Plan and says it is formulated for frail elderly patients or those in the terminal phase of illness, explicitly documenting preferences regarding cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR), place of care (home vs. acute hospital vs. hospice). The naming differs across sources.

- **Facilitators:** The programme relies on trained staff. An ACP facilitator is a trained healthcare professional who can guide your family and you through the discussion and document your preferences onto a form.

- **Substitute decision-makers:** Singapore's term is "substitute decision-maker" rather than surrogate or spokesperson. Individuals identify a substitute decision-maker and also establish the goals of care relevant to their disease stage. Under the Mental Capacity Act, the appointment can be informal or formal. There are two categories of substitute decision-makers: 1. Informally appointed substitute decision-maker. One or more person(s) can be chosen during the ACP process and the choice will be noted in the patient's medical records. The other category is a formally appointed substitute decision-maker, referred to as a donee. Another source notes that ACP is not legally binding in Singapore and, therefore, there is no need to engage a lawyer for its discussion or documentation.

- **Limits:** The sources describe the substitute decision-maker as acting only when the patient lacks capacity. The substitute decision-maker can only make decisions on the patient's behalf when the patient lacks the capacity for decision-making. If the patient still has the capacity, healthcare professionals will obtain consent from the patient.
