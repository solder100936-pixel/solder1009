## 工具回傳文字（原樣）
The search turned up several Korean "caregiver murder" (간병살인) cases and sentences, though nothing from Seoul Shinmun specifically and little on how police investigate these cases.

- **Sentences tend to be lenient when family hardship is the backdrop.** A husband and son who killed a wife/mother after a decade of care had their sentences of 3 and 7 years finalized by the Supreme Court in May 2026 (the Supreme Court's Third Division dismissed the appeals and upheld the lower court's sentences of three years in prison). In a separate Alzheimer's case, the Supreme Court upheld a three-year sentence for the father, and lower courts handed down lighter-than-statutory sentences after considering the family's circumstances.
- **Shorter terms for spousal killings.** A man in his 60s who killed his terminally ill wife received four years after caring for her for over five years, saying that he made a "horrible choice out of desperation". A 62-year-old man in Seoul received five years in a rare-disease spousal case, where he had taken over most of the care and quit his job and faced financial difficulties.
- **Courts still stress severity.** An 80-year-old who killed his dementia-stricken wife got three years, and the court said murder is a serious crime that cannot be justified for any reason. On appeal, the sentence stayed at three years despite both sides appealing (the same as the first trial. Previously, both the defendant and the prosecution appealed on the grounds of 'excessive sentencing.').
- **Son's case with heavier terms.** A son who killed his bedridden mother received seven years, and the appeals court said murder is a serious crime that infringes on human life. A study cited in that report found that 75.8 percent of the cases stemmed from a lack of familial support.
- **Investigation angle.** In the 80-year-old's case, the autopsy listed the cause of death as unknown, so the prosecution initially charged Mr. A with attempted murder and detained him. The charge was later changed to murder.

Gaps: these results are mostly English-language Asia Economy and Korea Times/JoongAng pieces. I found no Seoul Shinmun coverage, no national statistics on case counts, and no detail on police investigation procedures. A follow-up search in Korean (e.g., 간병살인 서울신문 통계) would be needed for those.
